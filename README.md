# CompStark

Fast, formally verified components of a STARK prover and verifier in Lean 4, benchmarked against [Plonky3](https://github.com/Plonky3/Plonky3).

CompStark builds on [CompPoly](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/CompPoly), which provides the fields, packed storage and transforms: KoalaBear in Montgomery form, its quartic extension, parallel NTTs and the batched coset low-degree extension. CompStark adds the protocol layers on top: hashing, Merkle commitments, FRI and the prover and verifier. Every fast implementation comes with a proof that it computes its specification, and with a benchmark against the corresponding Plonky3 component.

## Status

- Poseidon2 over KoalaBear, widths 16 and 24, with Plonky3's default constants (`CompStark/Poseidon2`). The specification runs on any field of characteristic `p`; tests check Plonky3's outputs. `permute_map` transports a run along any ring homomorphism, so the permutation on CompPoly's Montgomery field is the specification (`fast_width16`, `fast_width24`). The specification is [ArkLib](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/ArkLib)'s Poseidon2, a translation of leanSpec's, at both widths (`permute_arkLib16`, `permute_arkLib24`, `CompStark/Poseidon2/ArkLib.lean`); proving it found two indexing bugs in ArkLib's round constants, fixed in [ArkLib#1316](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/ArkLib/pull/1316), which CompStark pins through [ArkLib#1317](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/ArkLib/pull/1317), a Lean v4.35 bump stacked on the FRI soundness proof of [ArkLib#992](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/ArkLib/pull/992).

- Packed Poseidon2 (`CompStark/Poseidon2/Packed.lean`): eight states at a time, one per vector lane, with macro-generated round kernels on Montgomery words. `permute16_width16` and `permute24_width24` (`PackedCorrectness.lean`, `PackedCorrectness24.lean`, on the width-generic `PackedLanes.lean`) prove that on every group of eight states they compute Plonky3's width-16 and width-24 permutations, read in the spec field, and leave the rest of the buffer unchanged. The S-box cube leaves its square unreduced and reduces the product by sign, and the sum of a partial round is a tree beside its S-box. Single thread, on an 8-core Ryzen 7 3700X: about 189 ns per width-16 permutation and 289 ns per width-24 one, against 108 ns and 179 ns for Plonky3's AVX2-packed `Poseidon2KoalaBear` and 624 ns for its scalar width-16 one (`CompStarkBench poseidon2-packed 14`, `CompStarkBench perm24 14`, `bench/rust: compstark-bench bench 14`).
- Hashing and Merkle commitments (`CompStark/Hash/Sponge.lean`, `CompStark/Merkle/Tree.lean`): Plonky3's `PaddingFreeSponge` and `TruncatedPermutation`, and `MerkleTree::new` over lists of matrices of mixed heights, with the Poseidon2 instances of Plonky3's `Poseidon2MerkleMmcs` (width-24 sponge for leaves, width-16 compression). Tests check digests and roots against Plonky3, including odd heights and injected smaller matrices. For matrices of one height, the tree is [VCVio](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/VCVio)'s verified Merkle tree (`CompStark/Merkle/VCVio.lean`): the root is `buildMerkleTreeWithHash`'s, openings are `generateProof`'s paths read from the leaves up, honest openings verify, and two accepted openings of different rows at one index give a collision of the compression or of the leaf hash (`verifyBatch_binding`). VCVio is pinned at ArkLib's revision with small fixes for Lean v4.35's Mathlib.
- Packed Merkle commitment (`CompStark/Merkle/Packed.lean`): the root of one matrix given as chunks of rows, leaves hashed eight rows at a time by the packed width-24 sponge in parallel chunk tasks, layers compressed eight pairs at a time by the packed width-16 permutation in parallel range tasks. `root_eq` (`RootCorrectness.lean`, on `PackedCorrectness.lean`) proves it is `Merkle.KoalaBear.root` of that matrix. For $2^{14}$ rows of 64 elements: about 3.8 ms on 16 chunks and 25.6 ms on one core, against 2.1 ms and 15.6 ms for Plonky3's `MerkleTreeMmcs::commit` on its default rayon pool and on one thread (`CompStarkBench merkle 14 64 16`, `taskset -c 5 CompStarkBench merkle 14 64 1`, `bench/rust: compstark-bench merkle 14 64`, `RAYON_NUM_THREADS=1`).
- STARK prover and verifier (`CompStark/Stark/Prover.lean`, `CompStark/Stark/Verifier.lean`, on `CompStark/Challenger.lean`, `CompStark/Merkle/Mmcs.lean`, `CompStark/Fri/Prover.lean` and `CompStark/Fri/Verifier.lean`): Plonky3's `uni-stark` `prove` and `verify` with the two-adic FRI commitment scheme at Plonky3 revision `47e442f`, including FRI folds of higher arity, over KoalaBear and its quartic extension with the Poseidon2 Merkle commitment above and the width-24 duplex challenger. AIRs are lists of constraint expressions (`CompStark/Stark/Air.lean`). On Plonky3's proofs of a Fibonacci AIR and of a degree-3 AIR with a two-chunk quotient, for several FRI parameter sets (`tests/data`), the verifier accepts them and the prover reproduces them exactly, grinding witnesses included. Both are reference implementations: the prover's speed comes from the fast backend below. No preprocessed columns or zero knowledge yet.
- Fast prover backend (`CompStark/Stark/Fast.lean`): `proveWith` runs on a `Backend` of kernels: the transcript's permutation and grinding, low-degree extension, the Merkle digest layers, row access, the quotient values, opened values and rows batched by powers of `α`. The fast backend keeps matrices as packed Montgomery words, uses the packed Poseidon2 (eight grinding candidates per permutation as Plonky3, in parallel waves), CompPoly's batched coset LDE with plans prepared per proof shape (`backendFor`), the packed Merkle tree, vectorized word kernels (`CompStark/Stark/Words.lean`), extension vectors as coordinate planes with inverse denominators by the norm (`CompStark/Stark/ExtWords.lean`) and parallel tasks throughout, and reproduces Plonky3's proofs like the specification backend (`CompStarkBench prove fast AIR … JSON`). On the representative configuration, `wide 64` (64 columns, degree-3 transitions, two quotient chunks) with blowup 2, 100 queries and 16-bit query grinding, it is on par with Plonky3 on 16 threads: about 235 ms against 230 ms for $2^{16}$ rows, 82 ms against 72 ms for $2^{14}$, 0.87 s against 1.0 s for $2^{18}$; with one worker thread about 1.3 s against Plonky3's 1.9 s on one thread for $2^{16}$ (`CompStarkBench prove fast wide 16 1 0 1 100 0 16`, `bench/rust: compstark-bench prove-bench wide 16 1 0 1 100 0 16`, `LEAN_NUM_THREADS=1`, `RAYON_NUM_THREADS=1`; `CompStarkBench phases 16` and `compstark-bench prove-trace …` break the time down by phase). The fast prover produces the specification's proof: `prove_fast` (`CompStark/Stark/Fast/Refines.lean`) proves `proveWith (backendFor …) params air logN trace publicValues = prove params air logN trace publicValues` under explicit assumptions. On the input (`InputOk`, `CompStark/Stark/Refines.lean`): the trace has `2 ^ logN` rows of the AIR's positive width, the AIR reads only its own columns, `logN + logBlowup ≤ 24` so that the extension stays within KoalaBear's two-adic subgroups, the quotient's chunks fit the blowup, and the extended trace and quotient are addressable by machine indices. On the run: the specification's out-of-domain point `ζ` (`outOfDomainPoint`) lies outside the base field. The last one is the interesting one: for `ζ` in KoalaBear, which happens with probability about $2^{-93}$, some `ζ - x` vanishes, and the two backends compute different garbage from batch inversion and the norm. `proveWith_refines` reduces the theorem to laws of the backend's kernels (`Fri.Refines`), each stating the shapes it needs (`CompStark/Fri/Assumptions.lean`) and proved in `CompStark/Stark/Fast/`: the transcript, with the smallest grinding witness from the parallel waves, CompPoly's LDE, every Merkle layer and opening, the quotient, the opened values with inverses by the norm, the batched reduction and the folds; the generic proof carries the shapes through the FRI commit phase (`CompStark/Fri/Shapes.lean`). Packed values relate to the specification's by their encoding.
- Completeness (`CompStark/Completeness.lean`): if a trace satisfies the AIR, the honest prover's proof verifies:

  ```lean
  theorem completeness (config : ValidConfig params air logN)
      (luck : NoBadLuck params air logN trace publicValues) :
      air.Satisfied logN trace publicValues →
        verify params air (prove params air logN trace publicValues) publicValues = .ok ()
  ```

  `air.Satisfied` (`CompStark/Stark/Satisfied.lean`) says the trace is a `2 ^ logN × width` matrix on whose rows every constraint vanishes, with Plonky3's selector values there. `ValidConfig` collects the conditions on the configuration, checkable before proving: the constraints read only the AIR's columns, their degrees fit the quotient's chunks (`Air.DegreeFits`, which counts the transition selector as degree 1 where Plonky3's `degree_multiple` counts 0), the extended trace domain is a KoalaBear subgroup, the chunks fit the blowup, FRI folds at least once per round down to a final polynomial no longer than the trace, and makes at least one query. `NoBadLuck` collects the two unlikely events in which the honest run fails: the out-of-domain point `ζ` lands in the base field (probability about $2^{-93}$), or a proof-of-work search finds no witness, which the transcript records (`proveTranscript`, where Plonky3's grinder would panic). The proof (`CompStark/Complete/`) replays the prover's transcript in the verifier; the honest Merkle openings verify through the VCVio bridge; the batched DEEP quotients of the committed low-degree extensions are low degree and the verifier recomputes them at each query; FRI's folds keep low degree, for inputs of mixed heights, and each query's folds replay the prover's down to the final polynomial; and the quotient's chunks recompose at `ζ` to the folded constraints over `Z_H(ζ)`. Soundness: see below.
- Knowledge soundness (`CompStark/Soundness.lean`), of the STARK as an interactive oracle proof (`CompStark/Iop.lean`): the protocol behind `verify` with oracles in place of the Merkle commitments and uniform challenges in place of Fiat–Shamir. A prover is a trace word per column on the extension domain, quotient chunk words after `α`, and claimed values after `α` and `ζ`, followed by FRI on the batched DEEP word, where it may follow any strategy. The extractor list-decodes the committed trace: `closeTraces` are the traces whose columns are polynomials of degree at most `n` agreeing with the committed words on a common set of `(1 - δ') N` points. FRI tests proximity `δ`, and the extractor list-decodes at `δ' ≥ δ`.

  ```lean
  theorem knowledgeSoundness (S : Setup) (publicValues : List F) (prover : Prover S)
      (δ δ' : NNReal) (hδ' : (δ' : ℝ) < 1 - S.rate) :
      (∀ trace ∈ closeTraces S δ' prover, ¬ S.air.Satisfied S.logN trace publicValues) →
        acceptance publicValues prover ≤ knowledgeError S δ δ'
  ```

  A `Setup` is a valid configuration with a positive blowup and at least one FRI fold. The knowledge error (`CompStark/SoundnessError.lean`) has one term per challenge, with `ℓ` the list sizes of the trace's and the quotient's interleaved codes at `δ'` (`Setup.listSize`): `ℓ (#constraints - 1) / |𝕂|` for `α`, `(ℓ² · identityDegree + n) / |𝕂|` for `ζ` (Schwartz–Zippel on the constraint identity of each pair of candidates, and `ζ` landing on the trace domain), the correlated-agreement error of the powers of the batching challenge (the batched word `δ`-close although the words are not on a common set at `δ' - 2/N`, as decoding loses the up to two points of `L` where `ζ` or `ζ ω_n` lies; for `δ ≤ δ' - 2/N` at most the mutual correlated-agreement error at `δ`, `batchingError_le_mcaError`), and FRI's soundness error at `δ`, from ArkLib's proof of FRI's round-by-round soundness ([ArkLib#992](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/ArkLib/pull/992), after Garreta–Mohnblatt–Wagner). The theorem holds for every `δ` and every `δ'` below `1 - rate`; the error is small up to the Johnson bound `1 - √rate`, where the list sizes are, and below the unique-decoding radius `ℓ ≤ 1` (`listSize_le_johnson`, `listSize_le_one`, `CompStark/Sound/Johnson.lean`). Concrete security (`CompStark/Security.lean`): for the Fibonacci AIR on $2^{10}$ rows with blowup 16, FRI folding by arity 2 and 110 queries, the knowledge error with FRI at `δ = 15/32 - 2^{-12}` and the extractor at `δ' = 15/32 - 2^{-14}` is at most $2^{-100}$ (`fibonacci_knowledgeError_le`, about $2^{-100.29}$ exactly). Batching all opened words by powers of one challenge needs correlated agreement for curves: `mcaError_powers_le` (`CompStark/Sound/PowersMca.lean`) bounds it by `k · N / |𝕂|` below the unique-decoding radius, on [BCIKS20] Theorem 6.1 for curves, vendored in `CompStark/Sound/CurveBW.lean` from the open [ArkLib#1311](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/ArkLib/pull/1311) until it lands, and with a proximity loss `δ < δ'` the error is independent of `N` (`batchingError_le_curve`, `CompStark/Sound/CurveCA.lean`). FRI's folds use the same argument through ArkLib's FRI soundness from lossy correlated agreement: `friError` is ArkLib's lossy FRI error at the best proximity loss, and with a loss of $2^{-17}$ each fold onto a domain of at least $2^{16}$ points costs `max(T₁, δ_int / loss) / |𝕂|` independently of its size (`friError_le_lossy`, `CompStark/Sound/LossyMca.lean`), at the price of `#folds · loss` in the query term. With it, `wide 64` (64 columns, degree-3 constraints, 136 opened words) on $2^{20}$ rows, blowup 4, arity 2 and 149 queries has knowledge error at most $2^{-100}$ with FRI at `δ = 3/8 - 2^{-12}` and the extractor at `δ' = 3/8 - 2^{-16}` (`wide_knowledgeError_le`, about $2^{-100.26}$ exactly), dominated by FRI's queries and the out-of-domain term `(identityDegree + n) / |𝕂|`; on $2^{21}$ rows it takes 150 queries at the same radii (`wide21_knowledgeError_le`, about $2^{-100.16}$ exactly). Since that term grows with the list size squared, the extractor stays below the unique-decoding radius at this size. The non-interactive verifier (Merkle commitments, Fiat–Shamir with the Poseidon2 duplex challenger, grinding) is not covered yet.
## Building

```bash
lake exe cache get   # Mathlib's prebuilt oleans
lake build
lake test
```

The Rust side lives in `bench/rust`; `cargo run --release` prints the Poseidon2 known answers used by the tests, `compstark-bench challenger` the challenger's, and `compstark-bench stark AIR LOG_N LOG_BLOWUP LOG_FINAL MAX_LOG_ARITY QUERIES COMMIT_POW QUERY_POW` a proof of the `fib` or `cube` AIR as JSON. Run the last two with `RAYON_NUM_THREADS=1`, so that grinding returns the smallest witness. Lake does not track `tests/data`: after replacing a proof there, check it with `lake env lean tests/CompStarkTests/Stark.lean`.

## Later

- Custom externs for the Poseidon2 kernels. Every `ByteArray` store in Lean carries an exclusivity check, so a loop over the eight lanes stays scalar, and clang's SLP vectorizer only handles one round at a time. Written as one loop over the lanes, the whole width-24 permutation keeps its state in registers and vectorizes cleanly: 211 ns in a C model of the Lean kernels, against 289 ns for the split Lean kernels and 179 ns for Plonky3. A check-free store primitive would unlock that shape, at the price of trusting an extern.

## Roadmap

1. Plonky3's multi-table STARK: the verifier, then `batch-stark` with LogUp lookups and preprocessed columns.
2. Binary fields, following [leanVM](https://github.com/leanEthereum/leanMultisig): a binary tower field, WHIR, and Binius-style ring switching.
3. Multilinear proving, following [SP1 Hypercube](https://blog.succinct.xyz/sp1-hypercube/): zerocheck by sumcheck, LogUp-GKR, and Basefold behind the jagged commitment, over KoalaBear with Poseidon2.
