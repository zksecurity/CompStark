# CompStark

Fast, formally verified components of a STARK prover and verifier in Lean 4, benchmarked against [Plonky3](https://github.com/Plonky3/Plonky3).

CompStark builds on [CompPoly](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/CompPoly), which provides the fields, packed storage and transforms: KoalaBear in Montgomery form, its quartic extension, parallel NTTs and the batched coset low-degree extension. CompStark adds the protocol layers on top: hashing, Merkle commitments, FRI and the prover and verifier. Every fast implementation comes with a proof that it computes its specification, and with a benchmark against the corresponding Plonky3 component.

## Status

- Poseidon2 over KoalaBear, widths 16 and 24, with Plonky3's default constants (`CompStark/Poseidon2`). The specification runs on any field of characteristic `p`; tests check Plonky3's outputs. `permute_map` transports a run along any ring homomorphism, so the permutation on CompPoly's Montgomery field is the specification (`fast_width16`, `fast_width24`).

- Packed Poseidon2 (`CompStark/Poseidon2/Packed.lean`): eight states at a time, one per vector lane, with macro-generated round kernels on Montgomery words. `permute16_width16` and `permute24_width24` (`PackedCorrectness.lean`, `PackedCorrectness24.lean`, on the width-generic `PackedLanes.lean`) prove that on every group of eight states they compute Plonky3's width-16 and width-24 permutations, read in the spec field, and leave the rest of the buffer unchanged. The S-box cube leaves its square unreduced and reduces the product by sign, and the sum of a partial round is a tree beside its S-box. Single thread, on an 8-core Ryzen 7 3700X: about 189 ns per width-16 permutation and 289 ns per width-24 one, against 108 ns and 179 ns for Plonky3's AVX2-packed `Poseidon2KoalaBear` and 624 ns for its scalar width-16 one (`CompStarkBench poseidon2-packed 14`, `CompStarkBench perm24 14`, `bench/rust: compstark-bench bench 14`).
- Hashing and Merkle commitments (`CompStark/Hash/Sponge.lean`, `CompStark/Merkle/Tree.lean`): Plonky3's `PaddingFreeSponge` and `TruncatedPermutation`, and `MerkleTree::new` over lists of matrices of mixed heights, with the Poseidon2 instances of Plonky3's `Poseidon2MerkleMmcs` (width-24 sponge for leaves, width-16 compression). Tests check digests and roots against Plonky3, including odd heights and injected smaller matrices.
- Packed Merkle commitment (`CompStark/Merkle/Packed.lean`): the root of one matrix given as chunks of rows, leaves hashed eight rows at a time by the packed width-24 sponge in parallel chunk tasks, layers compressed eight pairs at a time by the packed width-16 permutation in parallel range tasks. `root_eq` (`RootCorrectness.lean`, on `PackedCorrectness.lean`) proves it is `Merkle.KoalaBear.root` of that matrix. For $2^{14}$ rows of 64 elements: about 3.8 ms on 16 chunks and 25.6 ms on one core, against 2.1 ms and 15.6 ms for Plonky3's `MerkleTreeMmcs::commit` on its default rayon pool and on one thread (`CompStarkBench merkle 14 64 16`, `taskset -c 5 CompStarkBench merkle 14 64 1`, `bench/rust: compstark-bench merkle 14 64`, `RAYON_NUM_THREADS=1`).
- STARK prover and verifier (`CompStark/Stark/Prover.lean`, `CompStark/Stark/Verifier.lean`, on `CompStark/Challenger.lean`, `CompStark/Merkle/Mmcs.lean`, `CompStark/Fri/Prover.lean` and `CompStark/Fri/Verifier.lean`): Plonky3's `uni-stark` `prove` and `verify` with the two-adic FRI commitment scheme at Plonky3 revision `47e442f`, including FRI folds of higher arity, over KoalaBear and its quartic extension with the Poseidon2 Merkle commitment above and the width-24 duplex challenger. AIRs are lists of constraint expressions (`CompStark/Stark/Air.lean`). On Plonky3's proofs of a Fibonacci AIR and of a degree-3 AIR with a two-chunk quotient, for several FRI parameter sets (`tests/data`), the verifier accepts them and the prover reproduces them exactly, grinding witnesses included. These are reference implementations, not yet fast or proved; no preprocessed columns or zero knowledge yet.
- Fast prover backend (`CompStark/Stark/Fast.lean`): `proveWith` runs on a `Backend` of kernels: the transcript's permutation and grinding, low-degree extension, the Merkle digest layers, row access, the quotient values, opened values and rows batched by powers of `α`. The fast backend keeps matrices as packed Montgomery words, uses the packed Poseidon2 (eight grinding candidates per permutation as Plonky3, in parallel waves), CompPoly's batched coset LDE with plans prepared per proof shape (`backendFor`), the packed Merkle tree, vectorized word kernels (`CompStark/Stark/Words.lean`), extension vectors as coordinate planes with inverse denominators by the norm (`CompStark/Stark/ExtWords.lean`) and parallel tasks throughout, and reproduces Plonky3's proofs like the specification backend (`CompStarkBench prove fast AIR … JSON`). On the representative configuration, `wide 64` (64 columns, degree-3 transitions, two quotient chunks) with blowup 2, 100 queries and 16-bit query grinding, it is on par with Plonky3 on 16 threads: about 235 ms against 230 ms for $2^{16}$ rows, 82 ms against 72 ms for $2^{14}$, 0.87 s against 1.0 s for $2^{18}$; with one worker thread about 1.3 s against Plonky3's 1.9 s on one thread for $2^{16}$ (`CompStarkBench prove fast wide 16 1 0 1 100 0 16`, `bench/rust: compstark-bench prove-bench wide 16 1 0 1 100 0 16`, `LEAN_NUM_THREADS=1`, `RAYON_NUM_THREADS=1`; `CompStarkBench phases 16` and `compstark-bench prove-trace …` break the time down by phase). The fast prover produces the specification's proof: `prove_fast` (`CompStark/Stark/Fast/Refines.lean`) proves `proveWith (backendFor …) P A logN trace pis = prove P A logN trace pis` under explicit assumptions. On the input (`InputOk`, `CompStark/Stark/Refines.lean`): the trace has `2 ^ logN` rows of the AIR's positive width, the AIR reads only its own columns, `logN + logBlowup ≤ 24` so that the extension stays within KoalaBear's two-adic subgroups, the quotient's chunks fit the blowup, and the extended trace and quotient are addressable by machine indices. On the run: the specification's opening point `ζ` (`zetaOf`) lies outside the base field. The last one is the interesting one: for `ζ` in KoalaBear, which happens with probability about $2^{-93}$, some `ζ - x` vanishes, and the two backends compute different garbage from batch inversion and the norm. `proveWith_refines` reduces the theorem to laws of the backend's kernels (`Fri.Refines`), each stating the shapes it needs (`CompStark/Fri/Assumptions.lean`) and proved in `CompStark/Stark/Fast/`: the transcript, with the smallest grinding witness from the parallel waves, CompPoly's LDE, every Merkle layer and opening, the quotient, the opened values with inverses by the norm, the batched reduction and the folds; the generic proof carries the shapes through the FRI commit phase (`CompStark/Fri/Shapes.lean`). Packed values relate to the specification's by their encoding.

## Building

```bash
lake exe cache get   # Mathlib's prebuilt oleans
lake build
lake test
```

The Rust side lives in `bench/rust`; `cargo run --release` prints the Poseidon2 known answers used by the tests, `compstark-bench challenger` the challenger's, and `compstark-bench stark AIR LOG_N LOG_BLOWUP LOG_FINAL MAX_LOG_ARITY QUERIES COMMIT_POW QUERY_POW` a proof of the `fib` or `cube` AIR as JSON. Run the last two with `RAYON_NUM_THREADS=1`, so that grinding returns the smallest witness. Lake does not track `tests/data`: after replacing a proof there, check it with `lake env lean tests/CompStarkTests/Stark.lean`.

## Later

- Custom externs for the Poseidon2 kernels. Every `ByteArray` store in Lean carries an exclusivity check, so a loop over the eight lanes stays scalar, and clang's SLP vectorizer only handles one round at a time. Written as one loop over the lanes, the whole width-24 permutation keeps its state in registers and vectorizes cleanly: 211 ns in a C model of the Lean kernels, against 289 ns for the split Lean kernels and 179 ns for Plonky3. A check-free store primitive would unlock that shape, at the price of trusting an extern.
