# Components and theorems

The detailed reference of what CompStark implements and proves, component by component: files, theorem names, assumptions and benchmark commands. The [README](../README.md) gives the overview.

- Poseidon2 (`CompStark/Poseidon2`): the specification `permute` runs on any field. A field's implementation of an instance is a permutation with a proof that it is the specification (`Poseidon2.Impl`, `CompStark/Poseidon2/Impl.lean`), and `Poseidon2Field F` gives Plonky3's instances of widths 16 and 24 with their implementations, on which Plonky3's hashes and challenger are built; the specification itself is the default implementation (`Poseidon2Field.ofSpec`). Plonky3's default constants over KoalaBear (S-box `x ^ 3`, `CompStark/Poseidon2/KoalaBear.lean`, the round constants Plonky3 regenerated with the Grain LFSR) and BabyBear (S-box `x ^ 7`, `CompStark/Poseidon2/BabyBear.lean`); tests check Plonky3's outputs. `permute_map` transports a run along any ring homomorphism, so the permutation on CompPoly's Montgomery field is the specification (`KoalaBear.fast_width16`, `BabyBear.fast_width16`, …). KoalaBear's and BabyBear's implementations are the packed kernels below, one state in lane 0 of a group, for any round constants of the field's shape (`CompStark/Poseidon2/PackedInstances.lean`: `Instances p` holds both widths' constants with their shape proofs, `Instances.field` and `Instances.packed` give their `Poseidon2Field` and packed kernels, `Instances.perm16_eq`, `Instances.perm24_eq`; `KoalaBear.Instances` and `BabyBear.Instances` in `CompStark/KoalaBear/Poseidon2.lean` and `CompStark/BabyBear/Poseidon2.lean`, whose default instances are Plonky3's, and `fixtures/CompStarkFixtures/Poseidon2Example.lean` holds the ones Plonky3's example `prove_prime_field_31` draws with `new_from_rng_128` from `SmallRng` seeded with `1`, generated by `compstark-bench keccak-example-constants`). The interpreter cannot run the packed kernels (CompPoly's inline C stores), so the known answers that use them run compiled (`tests/CompStarkNativeTests.lean`, the test driver); the prover and the benchmarks run them too. The specification is [ArkLib](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/ArkLib)'s Poseidon2, a translation of leanSpec's, at both widths (`permute_arkLib16`, `permute_arkLib24`, `CompStark/Poseidon2/ArkLib.lean`), at Plonky3's current KoalaBear constants, which ArkLib's parameters predate; proving it found two indexing bugs in ArkLib's round constants, fixed in [ArkLib#1316](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/ArkLib/pull/1316).

- Packed Poseidon2 (`CompStark/Poseidon2/Packed.lean`, `PackedKernels.lean`): eight states at a time, one per vector lane, with round kernels on Montgomery words that `Unrolled.lean` generates for a field's shape: its modulus `p = r · 2 ^ s + 1`, its S-box degree and the tails of its internal diagonals, whose entries after the ninth are `±2 ^ (-k)` and cost a shift and a subtraction (`mulInvPow2`). The word arithmetic (`Arithmetic.lean`, `ArithmeticLemmas.lean`), the models of the kernels (`PackedLanes.lean`) and the correctness of the round loops (`PackedCorrectness.lean`, `PackedCorrectness24.lean`: `permute16_spec`, `permute24_spec`) are generic over a 31-bit Montgomery field with a `PackedKernels p` instance; a field's module generates its kernels and proves each equal to its model with the shared tactics (`PackedKoalaBear.lean`, S-box `x ^ 3`; `PackedBabyBear.lean`, S-box `x ^ 7`), and `KoalaBear.Packed.permute16_width16`, `BabyBear.Packed.permute24_width24` and their siblings read Plonky3's instances in the spec field. Every kernel first writes eight words of its group back unchanged, so the compiler knows the buffer is exclusive and interleaves loads with stores instead of holding all loaded words until the first exclusivity check. The multiplier-bound S-box kernel leaves its powers below `2 p` (`cubeLazy`, `pow7Lazy`) and the `mat4` kernel reduces them; a partial round's sum is a tree beside its S-box, and coordinate `0` is `rest - y`. The round loops carry a buffer of fixed size and check the group and the constant tables once per permutation. A field's partial rounds are one loop of its own with the kernel inlined (`PackedKernels.partials16`, `partials24`, on `partialsLoop16`), so a partial round makes no call. Single thread, on an 8-core Ryzen 7 3700X: about 157 ns per width-16 permutation and 255 ns per width-24 one over KoalaBear, against 118 ns and 193 ns for Plonky3's AVX2-packed `Poseidon2KoalaBear` and 676 ns for its scalar width-16 one (`CompStarkBench poseidon2-packed 14`, `CompStarkBench perm24 14`, `bench/rust: compstark-bench bench 14`); over BabyBear about 243 ns and 410 ns on a loaded host, against 213 ns and 318 ns for Plonky3's AVX2-packed `Poseidon2BabyBear` and 148 µs for the specification on one state, which BabyBear's provers and verifier ran before (`CompStarkBench babybear-perm 14`).
- Hashing and Merkle commitments (`CompStark/Hash/Sponge.lean`, `CompStark/Merkle/Tree.lean`, `CompStark/Plonky3/Hash.lean`): Plonky3's `PaddingFreeSponge` and `TruncatedPermutation`, and `MerkleTree::new` over lists of matrices of mixed heights, with the Poseidon2 instances of Plonky3's `Poseidon2MerkleMmcs` (width-24 sponge for leaves, width-16 compression). Tests check digests and roots against Plonky3, including odd heights and injected smaller matrices. The tree is [VCVio](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/VCVio)'s Merkle tree with injected payloads (`MixedMerkleTree`, `CompStark/Merkle/VCVio.lean`, `CompStark/Merkle/Mixed.lean`): the leaves hash the rows of the tallest matrices, and the rows of a shorter matrix are injected at the depth of its height (`injNode`). Plonky3's single and pruned multi-openings are VCVio's climbs and walks at the injection schedule read off the height groups (`climb_eq`, `walkM_eq`, `sched_eq_find`), so VCVio's restoration of the paths of a multi-opening, its honest-opening theorems and its extraction from a cache of answered hash queries (`MixedMerkleTree.extract`, stable under cache growth that predicts no reachable digest) carry over (`CompStark/Merkle/MixedMulti.lean`, `CompStark/Merkle/ExtractMixed.lean`). For matrices of one height, the tree is VCVio's inductive Merkle tree: the root is `buildMerkleTreeWithHash`'s, openings are `generateProof`'s paths read from the leaves up, honest openings verify, and two accepted openings of different rows at one index give a collision of the compression or of the leaf hash (`verifyBatch_binding`). VCVio is pinned at the `merkle-mixed` branch of the bot fork, which adds these trees. Commitments are Merkle caps, Plonky3's `MerkleTreeMmcs` `cap_height` (`Fri.Params.capHeight`, `Stark.Cap`): the `2 ^ h` digests of the layer `h` levels below the root, `h` capped at the tree's levels, the root alone for `h = 0` (`Merkle.capOf`); the transcript observes a cap's digests in order (`capElems`), and a multi-opening is checked against the cap entry above each index (`Merkle.verifyMultiM`, which rejects a cap of another size and matrices injected above the cap; `verifyBatch_of_verifyMulti` and `verifyBatch_of_verifyMulti_mixed` give every query an accepted opening of the subtree under its cap entry).
- Packed Merkle commitment (`CompStark/Merkle/Packed.lean`): the root of one matrix given as chunks of rows, leaves hashed eight rows at a time by the packed width-24 sponge in parallel chunk tasks (the fast prover hashes its extension in place, in tasks over ranges of 512 rows, `leavesIn`), layers compressed eight pairs at a time by the packed width-16 permutation in parallel range tasks. `root_eq` (`RootCorrectness.lean`, on `PackedCorrectness.lean`) proves it is `Merkle.Poseidon2.root` of that matrix. For $2^{14}$ rows of 64 elements: about 3.8 ms on 16 chunks and 25.6 ms on one core, against 2.1 ms and 15.6 ms for Plonky3's `MerkleTreeMmcs::commit` on its default rayon pool and on one thread (`CompStarkBench merkle 14 64 16`, `taskset -c 5 CompStarkBench merkle 14 64 1`, `bench/rust: compstark-bench merkle 14 64`, `RAYON_NUM_THREADS=1`).
- STARK prover and verifier (`CompStark/Stark/Prover.lean`, `CompStark/Stark/Verifier.lean`, on `CompStark/Challenger.lean`, `CompStark/Merkle/Mmcs.lean`, `CompStark/Fri/Prover.lean` and `CompStark/Fri/Verifier.lean`): Plonky3's `uni-stark` `prove` and `verify` with the two-adic FRI commitment scheme at Plonky3 revision `a4982ec`, including FRI folds of higher arity, over any base field with a `BaseField` instance and any extension field of it with a `Challenge` instance (`CompStark/Stark/Challenge.lean`: coordinates, base embedding and a ring isomorphism with CompPoly's spec field over the base field), with the Poseidon2 Merkle commitment above and the width-24 duplex challenger. The fast backend runs over any fast extension of a Montgomery field: the quartic and KoalaBear's quintic. AIRs are lists of constraint expressions (`CompStark/Stark/Air.lean`); as in Plonky3 (`main_next_row_columns`), an AIR that does not read the trace's next row (`Air.mainNextRow`) has its trace opened at `ζ` only, and the verifier rejects a next row opened for it. The transcript is Plonky3's typed Fiat–Shamir layer: the STARK, the commitment scheme and FRI each observe a domain separator first (`CompStark/DomainSeparator.lean`: the protocol's name, the Keccak-256 hash of its interaction pattern and the instance's shape), the duplex challenger tags each absorb with its length, and besides the fold and query rounds the rounds of `ζ` and of the batching challenge may grind too. FRI opens its queries as Merkle multi-openings with pruned paths (`Merkle.verifyMultiM`, `CompStark/Merkle/Multi.lean`: an accepted multi-opening gives every query an accepted single opening, and honest pruned paths verify). Preprocessed columns follow Plonky3's `setup_preprocessed` and `verify_with_preprocessed`: an AIR carries its preprocessed trace (`Air.preprocessed`, `Air.preprocessedWidth`), constraints read its current and next rows (`Expr.prepCur`, `Expr.prepNext`), and `setup` computes its verifying key (`PreprocessedKey`: the width, the trace's log height and the Merkle cap of its low-degree extension). The transcript observes the preprocessed cap after the trace cap; the commitment scheme opens the preprocessed trace at `ζ`, and at `ζ ω` only if the AIR reads its next row (`Air.preprocessedNextRow`); the verifier rejects a key or openings that do not match the AIR's shape. It also rejects an empty quotient cap, and FRI an empty commit-phase cap, before observing them: observing nothing would merge two duplexing rounds of the challenger, and Plonky3 rejects such a cap when it checks the openings against it. Periodic columns follow Plonky3's `periodic_columns`: an AIR lists one period per column (`Air.periodic`, read by `Expr.periodic`), of a length that is a power of two dividing the trace's height; as a polynomial a column of period `p` is `f(Xⁿᐟᵖ)`, `f` its period's interpolant (`periodicPoly`). Periodic columns are neither committed nor opened: the domain separator carries their number, the verifier interpolates each at `ζ ^ (n / p)` (`periodicAt`), and the prover extends each period onto the quotient domain (`periodicOnQuotient`). With periodic columns Plonky3 sizes the quotient by the constraints' polynomial degrees on the trace (`Expr.degBound`, a periodic column of degree `n - n / p`) rather than by `degree_multiple`, so the chunk count depends on the trace length (`Air.logQuotientChunks`). On Plonky3's proofs of a Fibonacci AIR, of a degree-3 AIR with a two-chunk quotient, of an AIR with preprocessed columns, with and without next-row reads, and of AIRs with periodic columns (Plonky3's `PeriodicAir` and a degree-3 AIR whose quotient the periodic sizing halves), for several FRI parameter sets (`tests/data`), grinding at every site included, the verifier accepts them and the prover reproduces them exactly, grinding witnesses included. Both are reference implementations: the prover's speed comes from the fast backend below. No zero knowledge yet. The verifier is written once over its hash functions (`CompStark/Primitives.lean`: a challenger that observes and samples base elements, the Merkle leaf hash and compression, all in a monad): `verify` is `verifyWith (poseidon2 F)`, and the same code runs on random oracles. FRI folds by Plonky3's schedule for the input heights (`foldSchedule`), which proofs no longer carry.
- Base field (`CompStark/BaseField.lean`): the verifier, the specification prover and the whole analysis (soundness, the verifier on random oracles, the spec field and its error terms) are generic over a prime field `F` of odd characteristic with a `BaseField` instance: finiteness as a proposition (`Finite F`, so no executable enumerates the field; the analysis counts it by `Nat.card F`), a prime number `p = |F|` of elements (`card_prime`, so the characteristic is `p`, `ringChar_eq_card`), a canonical value `F → ℕ`, a bijection onto `[0, p)` for `p = |F|` (CompPoly's `CanonicalNat`, of which `sampleBits` keeps the low bits); two-adic generators `twoAdicGen k` of order `2 ^ k` for `k ≤ twoAdicity`, each the square of the next, so that `2 ^ twoAdicity ∣ p - 1`; and a coset shift `gen ≠ 0` with `gen ^ 2 ^ twoAdicity ≠ 1`. The domains of a configuration must be two-adic subgroups: `logN + logBlowup ≤ twoAdicity` (`ValidConfig`). The bias of `sampleBits` is bounded for `2 ^ b ∣ p - 1` (`CompStark/Sound/Sampling.lean`), which the query indices satisfy, and the grinding probability is `powFraction F bits = ((p - 1) / 2 ^ bits + 1) / p` for any `p`. CompPoly's fast Montgomery field `FastField p` of any prime with a `Mont32Field p` instance is a base field given its domain data, `Mont32BaseField p`: the two-adicity, the two-adic generators and the shift with their proofs (`CompStark/Mont32/BaseField.lean`); its field structure is CompPoly's, so CompPoly's fast quartic extension `Ext4 p` over any `Binomial4 p` is a challenge field (`CompStark/Mont32/Challenge.lean`). KoalaBear (`CompStark/KoalaBear/Field.lean`: Plonky3's shift `3` and `TWO_ADIC_GENERATORS`, two-adicity 24) and BabyBear (`CompStark/BabyBear/Field.lean`: Plonky3's shift `31` and `TWO_ADIC_GENERATORS`, two-adicity 27) are instances, with their quartic extensions; KoalaBear's quintic one is a challenge field too (`CompStark/KoalaBear/Challenge.lean`). Plonky3's primitives over any base field with Poseidon2 instances, the duplex challenger, the Poseidon2 Merkle hashes, `poseidon2`, `verify` and the specification prover `prove`, live in `CompStark/Plonky3/`, and the JSON proof format of Montgomery fields in `CompStark/Mont32/Json.lean`; Plonky3's proofs over BabyBear verify and are reproduced like KoalaBear's (`tests/CompStarkTests/BabyBear.lean`). The fast backend and the completeness proof are generic too (below); the concrete numbers (`CompStark/Calculator/Security.lean`) are KoalaBear's.
- Fast prover backend (`CompStark/Stark/Fast.lean`), over any 32-bit Montgomery field `FastField p` with its domains (`Mont32BaseField p`), any CompPoly fast extension of it (`FastExt p EF`, of any degree `d` and monic modulus, with its algebra id `Mont32.AlgebraId EF` and its inversion of coordinate planes `PlanesInv p EF`: the quartic extensions `Ext4 p` of `Binomial4 p`, KoalaBear's quintic `KoalaBear.Fast.Ext5`), Poseidon2 instances (`Poseidon2Field`) with permutations of packed groups of eight states (`PackedPoseidon2 p`, `CompStark/Poseidon2/PackedField.lean`; KoalaBear's vectorized kernels, or any field's implementation state by state, `PackedPoseidon2.ofLanes`) and CompPoly's compiled coset LDE kernels. Each field instantiates it in the CPU-targeted native library, where the compiler specializes the generic code with the field's Montgomery constants as immediates: `CompStark/KoalaBear/Fast.lean` and `CompStark/BabyBear/Fast.lean` (`backendFor`; over KoalaBear on any Poseidon2 instances `Instances.backendFor`, correct by `Instances.prove_fast`). `proveWith` runs on a `Backend` of kernels: the transcript's permutation and grinding, low-degree extension, the Merkle digest layers, row access, the quotient values, and the openings: the opened values and, for the batching challenge `α` drawn after them, the rows batched by powers of `α` (`Backend.openings`). The fast backend keeps matrices as packed Montgomery words by columns, in blocks of the trace's height (`PMat`), uses the packed Poseidon2 (eight grinding candidates per permutation as Plonky3, in parallel waves), CompPoly's coset LDE with plans and quotient tables prepared per instance shape (`backendFor logBlowup shapes`), column by column (`ldeColumns`: ranges of 256 rows of the trace transposed into columns, every column extended into its blocks, no transpose or join of the output; columns of $2^{20}$ rows and more in `columnChains` chains of tasks, so that the columns extended at once stay within the caches), the quotient values as coordinate planes in the extension's bit-reversed order, the order the quotient evaluates its rows in (`Backend.Q`, `QRel`), every coordinate of every quotient chunk extended by a task of its own from its column, gathered from its plane by bit reversal (`chunksWith`, `chunksWith_rel`), the packed Merkle tree, which commits to matrices of mixed heights by hashing the tallest ones' rows side by side, eight rows per packed group loaded from the columns by one vectorized load per block of sixteen columns, a partial block's other coordinates loaded from the group itself (`Merkle.Packed.hashColumns`, `CompStark/Merkle/PackedColumns.lean`), and injecting each smaller height into the layer of its height with one more packed compression (`commitLayers`; `commitLayers_eq` proves it is the specification's tree), CompPoly's vectorized word kernels (`Montgomery.Native32.Words`, `CompPoly/Fields/Montgomery/Native32Words.lean`, four products summed in 64 bits and reduced once per word, `Words.mulAdd4`: the opened values dot every column with the barycentric weights, and the batched rows multiply-add four columns at a time into four coordinate planes at a time, every column word read once per four planes, a last group of fewer planes one plane at a time (`Words.mulAdd4Into`), `Words.mulAdd4x4Into`), the openings' stored points and inverse denominators `1 / (z - x)` computed once per point on the tallest height, whose prefixes are the smaller heights', for both the opened values, every matrix's in a task of its own, and the reduction (`denoms`, `DenomsOk`), and the reduction summing per point its matrices' batched rows and values, one product by `1 / (z - x)` per point and row (`reduceRange`), FRI's folds of any arity as one kernel (`Backend.fold`, `Fri.foldOnce`: arity 2 on planes with the inverses of `2 x` of every layer computed once per backend (`foldTables`, `invTwoX`), larger arities group by group in parallel ranges, `lagrange` compiled with one inversion per point by the `csimp` lemma `lagrange_eq_lagrangeFast`), extension vectors as CompPoly's coordinate planes, generic over the modulus and the degree, with inverse denominators by the norm polynomial (`Montgomery.Native32.ExtPlanes`, `CompPoly/Fields/Montgomery/Native32ExtPlanes.lean`, proved in `Native32ExtPlanesCorrectness.lean`; inverses by the norm for the quartic, by Montgomery's batch inversion for the quintic) and parallel tasks throughout, and reproduces Plonky3's proofs like the specification backend (`CompStarkBench prove fast AIR … JSON`). On the representative configuration, `wide 64` (64 columns, degree-3 transitions, two quotient chunks) with blowup 2, 100 queries and 16-bit query grinding, Plonky3 is faster: on a quiet Ryzen 7 3700X about 0.12 s against Plonky3's 0.11 s for $2^{16}$ rows and 0.44 s against 0.41 s for $2^{18}$ on 16 threads, 0.85 s against 0.39 s and 2.9 s against 1.59 s on one thread (medians of three interleaved rounds) (`CompStarkBench prove fast wide 16 1 0 1 100 0 16`, `bench/rust: compstark-bench prove-bench wide 16 1 0 1 100 0 16`, `LEAN_NUM_THREADS=1`, `RAYON_NUM_THREADS=1`; `CompStarkBench phases quartic 16` and `compstark-bench prove-trace …` break the time down by phase). The fast prover produces the specification's proof: `prove_fast` (`CompStark/Stark/Fast/Refines.lean`) proves `proveWith (backendFor …) params air logN words publicValues = prove EF params air logN trace publicValues` for a trace given to the fast prover as row chunks of packed Montgomery words `words` that encode `trace` (`encoded : RT logN air.width words trace`, `CompStark/Stark/Fast/Rel.lean`), under explicit assumptions, for a field whose largest domains of extension elements are addressable (`DomainsAddressable`); `KoalaBear.prove_fast` and `KoalaBear.prove_fast_quintic` discharge it (`KoalaBear.backendForQuintic`, `KoalaBear.verifyFastQuintic`). Over the quintic extension the fast prover reproduces Plonky3's proofs (`tests/data/quintic_*.json`, `compstark-bench stark-quintic …`) and takes about 1.1 times its quartic time on `wide 64` and 1.03 to 1.13 times on Keccak-f, against Plonky3's 1.05 to 1.09 and 1.00 to 1.03 (`CompStarkBench prove fast-quintic wide …`, `CompStarkBench keccak-prove (quartic|quintic) LOG_N`, `compstark-bench prove-bench-quintic wide …`, `compstark-bench keccak-prove-bench (quartic|quintic) LOG_N`; the numbers are in the README). On the input (`InputOk`, `CompStark/Stark/Refines.lean`): the trace has `2 ^ logN` rows of the AIR's positive width, the AIR reads only its own columns, `logN + logBlowup ≤ twoAdicity` so that the extension stays within the two-adic subgroups, the quotient's chunks fit the blowup, and the extended trace and quotient are addressable by machine indices. On the run: the specification's out-of-domain point `ζ` (`outOfDomainPoint`) lies outside the base field. The last one is the interesting one: for `ζ` in the base field, which happens over KoalaBear with probability about $2^{-93}$, some `ζ - x` vanishes, and the two backends compute different garbage from batch inversion and the norm. `proveWith_refines` reduces the theorem to laws of the backend's kernels (`Fri.Refines`), each stating the shapes it needs (`CompStark/Fri/Assumptions.lean`) and proved in `CompStark/Stark/Fast/`: the transcript, with the smallest grinding witness from the parallel waves, CompPoly's LDE, every Merkle layer and opening, the quotient, the opened values with inverses by the norm, the batched reduction and the folds; the generic proof carries the shapes through the FRI commit phase (`CompStark/Fri/Shapes.lean`). Packed values relate to the specification's by their encoding. A backend takes the main trace in its own representation (`Backend.Trace`), for its extension and its permutation trace: the fast one takes row chunks of packed words, the form a parallel trace generator writes, and extends them column by column without joining them (`ldeWordsWith_rel`). The quotient's constraints compile once per AIR into a straight-line program (`Prog.compile`, `CompStark/Stark/Prog.lean`): a hash-consed DAG with constant folding, scheduled and allocated to registers by liveness, accumulating every constraint with the coordinates of its weight. The backend runs it on blocks of up to 1024 rows in the extension's bit-reversed order on the group machine (`Prog.groupMachine`, `CompStark/Stark/ProgGroup.lean`): the block's `d` accumulators and registers in one buffer, every instruction one call of a kernel that runs sixteen words at a time over the block in place, reading the block's rows copied from the extension's columns, and its next rows copied in one piece when they are consecutive, every accumulation adding the operand times the weight's `d` words to the `d` accumulators, four accumulators per pass over the block with their weights and offsets read once (`Prog.accPassesS` and its kin), multiplications with Plonky3's form of the Montgomery reduction (`Prog.mulM`: the high words of the product and of its quotient times `p`, subtracted; `mulM_eq` proves it is the field's multiplication); blocks of fewer than sixteen rows, or not a multiple of sixteen, run on the block interpreter (`Prog.runBlock`, `CompStark/Stark/ProgWords.lean`). The compiler is proved (`compile_run`: on a row, the program accumulates the constraints' values with their weights) and so are the group machine (`runGroup_rel`, `CompStark/Stark/Fast/ProgGroup.lean`: on every row of a block, it runs the program on the field's values) and the block interpreter (`runBlock_rel`), which give the quotient's law (`quotientWith_rel`). A program can also run as straight-line code generated for it (`CompStark/Stark/ProgGen.lean`): `#gen_group_chunks` evaluates the program during elaboration, cuts its code into chunks of about 2000 instructions, each one compiled function of kernel calls with the field's constants as immediates (`group_kernels p`, `CompStark/KoalaBear/Group.lean`), and checks each by the kernel's `rfl` equal to the code's run on the group machine: both reduce to the same kernel calls, so no kernel and no field arithmetic unfolds; chunks of one program can be generated in separate modules built in parallel, and `#gen_group_kernel` assembles them into a `Prog.GroupKernel`, with the program `Prog.compile cs` of the constraints `cs` it was generated from (`Fast.AirKernel`, evaluated once). `backendFor kernels` checks once that each kernel's code is its program's (`checkedKernels`), takes an AIR's program from the kernel generated for its constraints instead of compiling them, and runs any other program by the kernel whose code it is (`kernelFor`), the proofs unchanged (`prove_fast` and `proveBatch_fast` hold for any `kernels`). The Keccak fixture's kernel (`fixtures/CompStarkFixtures/KeccakKernel.lean`, 19 chunks in four modules) is checked against the AIR's program and used by the Keccak example's proof test and benchmarks. `verifyFast` (`CompStark/Stark/Fast/Verify.lean`; `KoalaBear.verifyFast`, `KoalaBear.Instances.verifyFast`) is Plonky3's verifier on a 32-bit Montgomery field with packed Poseidon2 kernels, computed faster and proved equal to `verify` (`verifyFast_eq`): the leaves of every Merkle multi-opening are hashed eight at a time in packed groups, and a query's batched quotient `∑ αⁱ (pᵢ(z) - pᵢ(x)) / (z - x)` of a matrix is `(∑ αⁱ pᵢ(z) - ∑ αⁱ pᵢ(x)) / (z - x)`, the opened values' sums computed once for all queries and the row's by multiplications by base elements.
- AIR fingerprints, the Keccak fixture and Plonky3's Keccak example. `Air.fingerprint` (`CompStark/Stark/Fingerprint.lean`) evaluates every constraint and every lookup of an AIR at one point of the quartic extension drawn from SplitMix64 and hashes them with the AIR's shape by Plonky3's Poseidon2 sponge, with a JSON report of one constraint per line; `compstark-bench fingerprint AIR LOG_N` (`bench/rust/src/fingerprint.rs`) computes the same from any Plonky3 `Air` through upstream's symbolic builder, and the tests match the example AIRs and Plonky3's `KeccakAir` (2633 columns, 3182 constraints) line for line. Plonky3's Keccak AIR is a fixture of the tests and benchmarks (`fixtures/CompStarkFixtures/Keccak.lean`); its traces are Plonky3's, as data. Trace generation is Clean's: [Clean](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/clean)'s CompStark backend (`backends/compstark`) proves its own keccak-air component, Plonky3's layout and constraints proved sound against Keccak-f, with CompStark from Clean's packed trace, and its keccak-air showcase checks the trace, the quotient kernel and the proofs against Plonky3's and benchmarks whole proofs. - Completeness (`CompStark/Complete/Stark.lean`): over any base field with Poseidon2 instances, if a trace satisfies the AIR, the honest prover's proof verifies (`KoalaBear.completeness` instantiates it):

  ```lean
  theorem completeness (config : ValidConfig params air logN)
      (luck : NoBadLuck EF params air logN trace publicValues) :
      air.Satisfied logN trace publicValues → publicValues.length = air.numPublicValues →
        verify params air (setup params air logN) (prove EF params air logN trace publicValues)
          publicValues = .ok ()
  ```

  The verifier checks that there are as many public values as the AIR declares (`Air.numPublicValues`), as Plonky3 does. `air.Satisfied` (`CompStark/Stark/Satisfied.lean`) says the trace is a `2 ^ logN × width` matrix on whose rows every constraint vanishes, with Plonky3's selector values there. `ValidConfig` collects the conditions on the configuration, checkable before proving: the constraints read only the AIR's columns (a next row only if the AIR declares it reads it), the preprocessed trace has `2 ^ logN` rows of the AIR's preprocessed width, the periodic columns' periods are powers of two dividing `2 ^ logN`, the constraints' degrees fit the quotient's chunks (`Air.DegreeFits`, which counts the transition selector as degree 1 where Plonky3's `degree_multiple` counts 0), the extended trace domain is a two-adic subgroup of the base field, the chunks fit the blowup, which is positive, FRI folds at least once per round down to a final polynomial no longer than the trace, and makes at least one query. `NoBadLuck` collects the two unlikely events in which the honest run fails: the out-of-domain point `ζ` lands in the base field (probability $p^{1-d}$ for an extension of degree $d$, about $2^{-93}$ for the quartic and $2^{-124}$ for the quintic one), or a proof-of-work search finds no witness, which the transcript records (`proveTranscript`, where Plonky3's grinder would panic). The proof (`CompStark/Complete/`) replays the prover's transcript in the verifier; the honest Merkle openings verify through the VCVio bridge; the batched DEEP quotients of the committed low-degree extensions are low degree and the verifier recomputes them at each query; FRI's folds keep low degree, for inputs of mixed heights, and each query's folds replay the prover's down to the final polynomial; and the quotient's chunks recompose at `ζ` to the folded constraints over `Z_H(ζ)`. The theorem holds for every challenge field `EF` with a `Challenge` instance (`CompStark/Stark/Challenge.lean`), such as `KoalaBear.Fast.Ext4` and `KoalaBear.Fast.Ext5`. Soundness: see below.

  On the interaction layer (`CompStark/Iop/StarkCompleteness.lean`), the honest prover of a satisfying trace (`Iop.Setup.honestProver`: the trace's and the quotient chunks' extensions onto `L` and their polynomials' values at `ζ` and `ζ ω_n`) is accepted by the oracle protocol on every execution whose out-of-domain point `ζ` avoids the base field (`Iop.Setup.completeness`, for a `ValidConfig`): the honest quotient makes the check at `ζ` hold, and DEEP quotients of codewords are codewords (`deepWord_mem_code`). FRI runs in Plonky3's coordinate, on the subgroup `⟨ω⟩` whose coset `L` is (`Setup.friDomain`); its codes there are those on `L`.
- Knowledge soundness (`CompStark/Iop/StarkSoundness.lean`), of the STARK as an oracle protocol on [ArkLib](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/ArkLib)'s interaction layer (`CompStark/Iop/Stark.lean`, on the setups and checks of `CompStark/Iop/StarkSetup.lean`): the protocol behind `verify` with oracles in place of the Merkle commitments and uniform challenges in place of Fiat–Shamir. The prover's first message is the trace length, a public move the verifier admits (`Statement.Length`), so that every later shape is a function of it; then the trace oracle, `α`, the quotient oracle, `ζ`, the claimed values in the clear, the batching challenge, and ArkLib's FRI on the batched DEEP word along Plonky3's schedule (`Fri.Interaction.protocol`, `Setup.friSchedule`). The verifier holds the extension of the AIR's preprocessed trace as words of its own (`Setup.preprocessedWord`), reads the DEEP word at a point through a query program on the trace and quotient oracles (`Setup.readDeep`), and accepts if FRI accepts and the constraints hold at `ζ`. The prover is any strategy; an `Iop.Prover`, words and messages as functions of the challenges, plays with FRI's honest prover (`Prover.strategy`) and is accepted on every run where its claimed values pass the check and its DEEP word has low degree (`Prover.strategy_accepts`). The extractor list-decodes the committed trace: `closeTraces` are the traces whose columns are polynomials of degree at most `n` agreeing with the committed words on a common set of `(1 - δ') N` points. FRI tests proximity `δ`, and the extractor list-decodes at `δ' ≥ δ`. `Statement.farAcceptance` is the probability that the verifier accepts while no trace close to the committed one satisfies the AIR at the claimed length:

  ```lean
  theorem knowledgeSoundness (C : Statement F) (publicValues : List F)
      {OutP : (C.protocol EF).tree.ExecutionPath → Type}
      (prover : Interaction.Oracle.Prover.Strategy unifSpec (C.protocol EF).tree
        (C.protocol EF).roles OutP)
      (δ δ' : ℕ → NNReal) (hδ' : ∀ n, C.Admits n → (δ' n : ℝ) < 1 - C.rate) :
      C.farAcceptance EF publicValues δ' prover ≤ C.knowledgeError EF δ δ'
  ```

  The statement (`Statement`: FRI parameters and an AIR reading only its columns) has no trace length: the prover claims one the verifier admits (`Statement.Admits`: `SoundConfig`, the verifier's checks on the length and the parameters, and the preprocessed trace's length) and the protocol is the STARK there (`Statement.setupAt`), and the bound is the largest knowledge error over the admitted lengths (`Statement.knowledgeError`), every admitted length covered, FRI's unfolded one included. The proof is a round-by-round knowledge certificate (`CompStark/Iop/StarkKnowledge.lean`): the doomed-transcript states of `CompStark/Sound/Rbr.lean` for `α`, `ζ` and `a` (`AlphaBad`, `ZetaBad`, `BatchBad`, each with its error term below), with the trace oracle mapped to the extracted trace, appended with `KnowledgeCertificate.append` to ArkLib's FRI certificate on the DEEP word (`Fri.Interaction.certificate`), gated by the constraint check at `ζ` (`gateCertificate`); ArkLib's `executeStrategies_extraction_le` turns it into the bound at each length (`Setup.extraction_le_knowledgeError`), and whatever length the prover sends, the certificate there bounds the rest. FRI's error is ArkLib's (`friErrorWith_eq`). The knowledge error at a length (`CompStark/Iop/StarkError.lean`) has one term per challenge, with `ℓ` the list sizes of the committed words' interleaved codes at `δ'` (`Setup.listSize`): `ℓ (#constraints - 1) / |𝕂|` for `α`, `(ℓ · identityDegree + n + N) / |𝕂|` for `ζ` (Schwartz–Zippel on the constraint identity of each candidate of the committed words, and `ζ` landing on the trace domain or on `L`, where the DEEP quotients divide by zero, and so then does `ζ ω_n`, `L` being a coset of a group holding `ω_n`; decoding finds the trace, permutation trace and quotient polynomials on one common set, so the candidates are those of the committed words as one family, `Setup.Committed`, and the term is linear in the list size, as in Haböck's DEEP-ALI analysis, <https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1216>, Theorem 8), the correlated-agreement error of the powers of the batching challenge (the batched word `δ`-close although the words are not on a common set at `δ'`; for `δ ≤ δ'` at most the mutual correlated-agreement error at `δ`, `batchingError_le_mcaError`), and FRI's soundness error at `δ` (`Fri.Interaction.error`, the error of ArkLib's interaction-layer FRI certificate, from ArkLib's FRI query and folding analysis of [ArkLib#992](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/ArkLib/pull/992), after Garreta–Mohnblatt–Wagner, with injected inputs). FRI's tree is Plonky3's commit phase: each fold round is the prover's word at the layer, the first being the batched DEEP word, then the folding challenge; the last layer has no word, only the final polynomial; and each query checks every committed word against the verifier's word there, the DEEP word computed from the trace and quotient openings at the first layer and the fold of the previous committed word after. The theorem holds for every `δ` and every `δ'` below `1 - rate`; the error is small up to the Johnson bound `1 - √rate`, where the list sizes are, and below the unique-decoding radius `ℓ ≤ 1` (`listSize_le_johnson`, `listSize_le_one`, `CompStark/Sound/Johnson.lean`). Concrete security (`CompStark/Calculator/Security.lean`): for the Fibonacci AIR with blowup 16, FRI folding by arity 2 and 110 queries, at every trace length the verifier admits (up to $2^{20}$ rows), the knowledge error with FRI at `δ = 15/32 - 2^{-15}` and the extractor at `δ' = 15/32 - 2^{-19}`, below the unique-decoding radius $15/32$, is at most $2^{-98}$ (`fibonacci_knowledgeError_le`, about $2^{-98.95}$ exactly on $2^{20}$ rows, $2^{-100.37}$ on $2^{10}$). Batching all opened words by powers of one challenge needs correlated agreement for curves: ArkLib bounds it by `k · N / |𝕂|` below the unique-decoding radius (`ProximityGap.exists_exactPowerAgreement_unique` with `ProximityGap.mcaError_powers_le_of_exactPowerAgreement`, on [BCIKS20] Theorem 6.1 for curves; `McaBound.uniqueDecoding_holds`), and with a proximity loss `δ < δ'` the error is independent of `N` (`batchingError_le_curve`, from ArkLib's `CodingTheory.UniqueDecoding.card_goodCoeffs_le`). FRI's folds use the same argument through lossy correlated agreement, at any arity: `friError` is the lossy FRI error at the best proximity loss, and a fold by $2^k$ onto a domain `L'` costs `(2^k - 1) min(B, |L'|) / |𝕂|` (one more word if it injects an input), with `B = max(T₁, δ_int / loss)` independent of `|L'|` for `δ ≤ (1 - rate) / 2 - 1/|L'|` and the powers MCA bound `|L'|` otherwise (`friErrorWith_le`, `lossyLevel_le`, from ArkLib's `CodingTheory.UniqueDecoding.lossyMcaError_le`), at the price of `#folds · loss` in the query term. With it, `wide 64` (64 columns, degree-3 constraints, 136 opened words) with blowup 4, arity 2 and 150 queries has knowledge error at most $2^{-98}$ at every admitted length, up to $2^{22}$ rows, with FRI at `δ = 3/8 - 2^{-12}` and the extractor at `δ' = 3/8 - 2^{-14}` (`wide_knowledgeError_le`, about $2^{-98.71}$ exactly on $2^{22}$ rows, $2^{-100.17}$ on $2^{20}$), dominated by the out-of-domain term `(identityDegree + n + N) / |𝕂|`, about $2^{-98.95}$ on $2^{22}$ rows, and FRI's queries. Since that term grows with the list size, the extractor stays below the unique-decoding radius at this size. Every term but the queries is an integer over $|\mathbb{K}|$, so the quartic extension ($p^4 \approx 2^{124}$) caps every configuration near 100 bits at these sizes (the interactive error cannot credit grinding, which only costs a prover that pays for its oracle calls; on Plonky3's verifier below, grinding lifts the cap to about 118 bits per duplex call); the analysis is generic in the extension field, and over the quintic one ($p^5 \approx 2^{155}$) `wide 64` with blowup 4 and 190 queries has knowledge error at most $2^{-128}$ at every admitted length (`wide5i_knowledgeError_le`, about $2^{-128.22}$ on $2^{22}$ rows). The non-interactive verifier is covered in the random-oracle model, next.
  State restoration (`CompStark/Iop/StarkStateRestoration.lean`, `CompStark/Iop/BatchStateRestoration.lean`): the STARK and the batch STARK are public-coin (`Statement.publicCoin`: the lengths the prover's first move, every challenge with one continuation, FRI's presentation `Fri.Interaction.publicCoin` appended), their certificates with uniform query positions sample every challenge uniformly, and the verifier's checks are a program of the branch (`Statement.terminal`). ArkLib's `KnowledgeCertificate.stateRestoration_extraction_le` then bounds the state-restoration game, where the adversary queries a random oracle for each challenge at its erased prefix and chooses the public values and every message: the checks pass while no trace of the claimed length close to the committed one satisfies the AIR with probability at most `Q · maxError + sumError` of the node errors (`Statement.stateRestoration_le`): at each length the folding, out-of-domain and batching errors, each FRI fold's error and FRI's query error at the length's proximity loss, whose path sum is the interactive error at that loss (`Statement.pathError_nodeErrors`). For the batch, a node's error is the largest over the instances (`Batch.Setup.nodeErrors`). The random-oracle theorems below are proved directly on Plonky3's verifier and do not go through this game.
- Knowledge soundness of Plonky3's verifier as it runs (`CompStark/Fs/StarkBcs.lean`), from ArkLib's BCS theorem (`KnowledgeCertificate.bcs_knowledge_soundness`): `verifyWith` itself, with Merkle commitments, Fiat–Shamir and grinding, run on random oracles in place of Poseidon2's three roles (`CompStark/Fs/RandomOracle.lean`): the duplex challenger, which takes the capacity and the elements observed since the last squeeze and answers a new capacity and `K` samples; the Merkle leaf hash; and the compression. The statement is a `Statement`, FRI parameters and an AIR with no trace length: the verifier takes the length from the proof, as Plonky3's does, and the theorem covers every length it admits (`Statement.Admits`), with the STARK at each (`Statement.setupAt`). The game (`Statement.starkGame`) computes the verifying key of the AIR's preprocessed trace on the oracles, runs an adversary that outputs public values and a proof, and runs Plonky3's verifier on them, all against one logged lazy random oracle (ArkLib's `Bcs.realImpl`, the verifier's oracles read as a duplex chain and two Merkle hashes by `translate`, `CompStark/Fs/HashChain.lean`). The extractor reads the trace off the log's hash answers under the proof's trace commitment (`traceMessageOf`, `merkleCache`, `hashCacheOf`):

  ```lean
  theorem knowledgeSoundness_bcs (h8 : 2 ^ 8 ≤ Nat.card F) (h64 : twoAdicity F < 2 ^ 64)
      (hK : 0 < K) (δ δ' : ℕ → ℝ≥0) (hδ' : ∀ n, C.Admits n → (δ' n : ℝ) < 1 - C.rate)
      (loss : C.Length → ℝ≥0) (adversary : OracleComp (spec F K) (List F × Proof F EF)) {Q q : ℕ}
      (hQ : IsQueryBoundP adversary (fun t => isDuplex t = true) Q)
      (hq : IsTotalQueryBound adversary q) :
      Pr{let z ← (simulateQ Bcs.realImpl
          (simulateQ translate (C.starkGame EF K adversary))).run []}[
        z.1.2.2.2 = .ok () ∧ ∀ h : C.Admits z.1.2.2.1.degreeBits,
          ∀ trace ∈ closeTraces (C.setupAt EF _ h) (δ' z.1.2.2.1.degreeBits)
            ((C.setupAt EF _ h).traceWords ((C.setupAt EF _ h).traceMessageOf
              (merkleCache (Bcs.hashCacheOf z.2)) z.1.2.2.1.traceCommit)),
          ¬ C.air.Satisfied z.1.2.2.1.degreeBits trace z.1.2.1] ≤
        chainError (Digest F) (Q + C.verifierQueries EF + 3 * C.verifierChainQueries EF K) +
          hashBadError F (2 ^ C.params.capHeight) (C.capOffsets EF).length
            (q + C.verifierQueries EF) +
          (maxError (fun (_ : C.Input) node ↦
              C.ratio EF node * C.nodeErrors EF δ δ' loss (1 / Nat.card F) node) *
              (Q + C.verifierQueries EF + 3 * C.verifierChainQueries EF K : ℕ) +
            sumError (fun (_ : C.Input) node ↦
              C.ratio EF node * C.nodeErrors EF δ δ' loss (1 / Nat.card F) node))
  ```

  The protocol is the STARK's public-coin presentation with every challenge drawn as Plonky3's verifier draws it (`Fs/Draws.lean`: `extDraw` for an extension element, after a grinding sample if the round grinds and with no witness at `0` bits, `queryDraw` for the query positions, each the low bits of one sample), from the answers of the challenge's squeezes, `K` samples each: a challenge needing more samples than one squeeze holds squeezes again with the empty input. Its layout (`Setup.layout`, `Statement.layout`, `CompStark/Fs/Stark.lean`) is what the verifier observes before each challenge, as Plonky3 observes it: the domain separators, the public values, the claimed values, the final polynomial, the grinding witnesses, and each committed word's Merkle cap, `2 ^ min capHeight depth` digests, opened to the word read off a snapshot of the hash answers under it (`merkleCache`, `StarkWords.lean`). The observations are prefix-free (`Statement.layout_prefixFree`) through the domain separator's length bytes (`DomainSeparator.starkSeedOf_inj`, which needs the challenger's byte chunks of `⌊log₂ p / 8⌋ ≥ 1` bytes and lengths within the domain separator's eight bytes: `h8`, `h64`). Each draw is bounded by its ratio against the law the knowledge certificate samples at its node (`Statement.bounded_decoder`): the grinding's `powFraction F bits`, with FRI's query positions drawn from the law Plonky3's verifier draws them from, the low bits of uniform base elements (`Setup.queryPositions`, slack `1/p`), so the positions' bias is paid inside the query error, `(1 - δ + #folds · loss + 1/p) ^ numQueries`. The node errors are the state-restoration theorem's (`Statement.nodeErrors`), each times its draw's ratio.

  The BCS theorem's adversary runs the game with its own log, reads the proof's selection off it (`Statement.parse`, `CompStark/Fs/StarkParse.lean`) and fills its snapshots from the log (`Statement.bcsAdversary`). On a run without digest clashes (`HashBad`: a hash answer equal to a digest known before it), extraction under an absorbed cap is the same from the cap's snapshot and from the whole log (`extractRow_snapshotAt`, `CompStark/Fs/MerkleLog.lean`, `opensStable_parse`), and an accepted opening opens the extracted rows (`capBinding_of_log`), so the verifier's acceptance gives the STARK's terminal on the extracted words (`Setup.terminal_of_verifyWith`, `CompStark/Fs/StarkTerminal.lean`) while a far trace makes the extraction fail (`Statement.bcsEvent_of_mem_support`). Digest clashes cost `hashBadError F capLen m q = (2 + m capLen) q² / |F|⁸` (`prEvent_hashBad_le`), `m` the offsets at which the verifier's inputs carry caps (`capOffsets`, `twoAdicity F + 3` of them) and `capLen = 2 ^ capHeight`; the capacities' collisions and predictions cost ArkLib's `chainError (Digest F) Q' = ⌊Q' (Q' + 1) / 2⌋ / |F|⁸`. `verifierQueries` counts the setup's and the verifier's oracle calls on a proof of any length (`verifierCallsAt`, `isTotalQueryBound_verifyWith`, `CompStark/Fs/StarkCalls.lean`), the setup's `2 · |L| - 1` calls none without preprocessed columns, and `verifierChainQueries` the duplex calls of the verifier's completion, at most `maxChallenges = twoAdicity F + 4` challenges (`Setup.challengesLE_publicCoin`) of at most `coinCount` squeezes each, which the BCS theorem's adversary runs once and ArkLib's simulation charges twice more, hence `Q' = Q + verifierQueries + 3 verifierChainQueries`. The theorem does not cover Poseidon2 itself or Plonky3's sponge and duplex constructions (rate/capacity chaining, length-tagged absorption, no padding): it idealizes each role of the hash as a random oracle.

  Each node error times its ratio is one of the security calculator's round terms (below; `Setup.ratio_mul_nodeErrors_le`, `CompStark/Calculator/FiatShamir.lean`), so the round error's bound (`Statement.roundErrorBound`) is the error per duplex call, and a path of at most `maxChallenges` challenges sums to at most `maxChallenges` times it: the calculator's `C.SecureRo EF p B A bits` gives `chainError Q' + hashBadError + (Q' + maxChallenges) · 2 ^ (-bits)` (`Statement.knowledgeSoundness_bcs_of_secureRo`), which `Statement.bcsBound` evaluates with the round error's bound in place of `2 ^ (-bits)`. The verifier's duplex calls count both in `Q'` and in the path sum. Per duplex call, Plonky3's Keccak example has `52.11` bits on `2 ^ 23` rows, `wide 64` with `wideGrindParams` `117.37`, and with SP1's parameters `98.95` at its longest admitted length; at `Q = q = 2 ^ 64` calls and `K = 8`, the bounds are about $2^{-53.37}$ for `wideGrindStatement`, $2^{-34.95}$ for SP1's parameters and $2^{-64.49}$ for `wide5Statement`, `Q'` times the per-call error dominating the capacities' $2^{-120.91}$ and the digest clashes' $2^{-115.05}$. `tests/CompStarkTests/Security.lean` instantiates the theorem at `117` bits (`wideGrind_knowledgeSoundness_bcs`), and `tests/CompStarkTests/KeccakSecurity.lean` for Keccak at `128` and, beyond the unique-decoding radius, `100` bits (`knowledgeSoundness_bcs_quintic`, `johnson_knowledgeSoundness_bcs_quintic`).

- Completeness of the batch STARK (`CompStark/Complete/Batch.lean`): over any base field with Poseidon2 instances, if every instance's trace satisfies its AIR, every bus balances and the multiplicities stay below the characteristic, the honest prover's batch proof verifies against the batch's common data (`KoalaBear.batchCompleteness` instantiates it):

  ```lean
  theorem Batch.completeness (config : Batch.ValidConfig params instances)
      (luck : Batch.NoBadLuck EF params instances traces publicValues) :
      Batch.Satisfied instances traces publicValues →
        verifyBatch params (instances.map (·.air)) (setupBatch params instances)
          (proveBatch EF params instances traces publicValues) publicValues = .ok ()
  ```

  `Batch.Satisfied` gives one trace and one list of public values per instance, every trace satisfying its AIR (`Air.Satisfied`) with as many public values as the AIR declares, every bus balanced (`Batch.Balanced`: for every bus and tuple of values, the multiplicities of the batch's messages, `Batch.messages`, sum to zero in the field) and the verifier's multiplicity bound. `Batch.ValidConfig` asks for at least one instance and a `ValidConfig` for every instance, every instance's quotient, LogUp constraints included, within the blowup and its constraints within the chunks, row-local lookups reading the AIR's columns (`Lookup.Fits`) and one tuple width per lookup and per bus; as the instances share FRI's parameters, FRI's final polynomial is then no longer than the shortest trace; and no instance's extension lies above another's Merkle cap (`capFits`), since the verifier rejects matrices injected above the cap. `Batch.NoBadLuck` is `NoBadLuck` for the batch's run (`batchOutOfDomainPoint`, `proveBatchTranscript`), plus no pole: under the run's lookup challenges no tuple of non-zero multiplicity has a zero denominator (`NoPoleAt`, probability `1/|EF|` per bus and tuple). The proof (`CompStark/Complete/Batch.lean`) replays the transcript and reads every instance's opened values as `uni-stark`'s honest openings, so each instance's constraint check passes as in `completeness`; the opening argument over matrices of mixed heights is complete for any honest rounds (`verifyPcs_openPcs`, `CompStark/Complete/Pcs.lean`): the Merkle trees with injected matrices open honestly (`verifyMulti_prune_mixed`, `CompStark/Merkle/Mixed.lean`), the batched quotients at a query are recomputed with every matrix's row at its own height, and FRI rolls each shorter input in when its folds reach that height (`verifyFri_proveFri`, `Sched`, `foldSchedule_sched`). For lookups (`CompStark/Complete/LogUp.lean`, `CompStark/Complete/LogUpQuotient.lean`), the honest permutation trace satisfies the LogUp constraints on every row (`logUpConstraints_honest`: a fraction pin `U f - V` vanishes on `f = ∑ m / d` when no tuple of non-zero multiplicity has `d = 0`), its column polynomials over the spec extension make every LogUp constraint `Z_H` times a quotient (`extPoly_eq`), the prover's quotient values are the constraints' folded quotient times `α ^ L` plus the LogUp constraints' (`toSpec_batchQuotientValues`), so every instance's check passes (`instanceCheck_honest`), and the terminals sum to the batch's messages weighted by their denominators, zero when every bus balances (`terminals_sum_eq_zero`).

  On the interaction layer (`CompStark/Iop/BatchCompleteness.lean`), a batch prover's strategy is accepted whenever the checks in the clear pass and every domain's batched DEEP word is a codeword on that domain `g ⟨ω_m⟩` (`Iop.Batch.Prover.strategy_accepts`): a codeword on a coset is one on every coset of the same subgroup, so FRI injects each shorter word in its code (ArkLib's `inputsInCode_plonky3`). The honest prover of a batch's traces (`Iop.Batch.Setup.honestProver`: every instance's trace, permutation trace and quotient chunks extended onto its domain, its terminal, and their polynomials' values at `ζ` and `ζ ω_n`, over the specification's layout of the lookup challenges) meets both conditions: for a `Batch.ValidConfig` and traces satisfying the batch (`Iop.Batch.Setup.Satisfied`), it is accepted on every execution whose `ζ` avoids the base field and under whose lookup challenges no denominator vanishes (`Iop.Batch.Setup.completeness`, `Setup.NoPole`). - Knowledge soundness of the batch STARK (`CompStark/Iop/BatchSoundness.lean`), as an oracle protocol on ArkLib's interaction layer (`CompStark/Iop/Batch.lean`, on `CompStark/Iop/BatchSetup.lean`): `k ≥ 1` AIRs, instance `i` on `2 ^ logN i` rows (`Batch.Setup`), each a STARK of its own (`Setup.instanceSetup`) on its own domain, under one `α`, one `ζ` and one batching challenge. The prover sends the lengths, every instance's trace as one oracle, the permutation traces as one oracle and the terminals of the instances with lookups (`Setup.Terminals`, as Plonky3's proof carries them) after the lookup challenges, the quotients after `α` and the claimed values after `ζ`. As Plonky3's commitment scheme reduces the openings by height, the DEEP quotients of the instances of each domain size are batched by the powers of the batching challenge, starting over at each size, in Plonky3's order (the main traces, the quotients, the preprocessed traces; `groupDeepWord`), and ArkLib's FRI runs on the tallest batched word and injects each shorter one when its folds reach that size (`Setup.friHeights`); the verifier reads a batched word at a point through the values of the instances of that size (`Setup.readGroupDeep`). The extractor outputs one trace per instance, each list-decoded from its committed trace (`Batch.closeTraces`), and `Batch.Statement.farAcceptance` is the probability that the verifier accepts while no close tuple satisfies a relation `R`:

  ```lean
  theorem batchKnowledgeSoundness (C : Batch.Statement F) (publicValues : Fin C.k → List F)
      {OutP : (C.protocol EF).tree.ExecutionPath → Type}
      (prover : Interaction.Oracle.Prover.Strategy unifSpec (C.protocol EF).tree
        (C.protocol EF).roles OutP)
      (δ δ' : (Fin C.k → ℕ) → NNReal) (hδ' : ∀ n, C.Admits n → (δ' n : ℝ) < 1 - C.rate) :
      C.farAcceptance EF publicValues δ'
        (fun n traces ↦ (C.setup EF n).Satisfied traces publicValues) prover ≤
        C.knowledgeError EF δ δ'
  ```

  As for the STARK, the batch statement (`Batch.Statement`: FRI parameters, the AIRs and their keygen heights, below) has no trace lengths: the prover claims a vector the verifier admits, and the bound is the largest knowledge error over the admitted vectors (`Batch.Statement.knowledgeError`). The certificate (`CompStark/Iop/BatchKnowledge.lean`) appends the doomed-transcript states of `CompStark/Sound/BatchRbr.lean` (`LookupBad`, then the failing instance's `AlphaBad`, `ZetaBad` and `BatchBad` with that instance's errors, so that each path's errors sum to its instance error) to ArkLib's FRI certificate on the batched words, gated by the terminal sum and the constraint checks.

  `Setup.Satisfied` asks every trace to satisfy its AIR and every bus to balance in the field (`Setup.Balanced`, over the batch's messages `Setup.messages`); the extractor outputs the main traces only, the permutation traces are auxiliary. The verifier first draws the lookup challenges `(α_L, β_L)` and accepts only if the instances' terminals sum to zero. If every close tuple satisfying every AIR leaves a bus unbalanced, the pair is bad with probability at most `lookupError`: for each such tuple the messages' fractions, with every denominator `α_L + (b + 1) β_Lᵂ - ∑ⱼ tⱼ β_Lʲ` cleared, give a nonzero polynomial of degree at most `T + (T - 1) W` in `(α_L, β_L)` over the `T` interactions of width at most `W`, and a pole of any tuple, of zero multiplicity or not, counts as bad too (Schwartz–Zippel, `CompStark/Sound/LogUp.lean`, `CompStark/Sound/BatchLogUp.lean`); Plonky3's `p3-security` estimates `N (W + 2) / |EF|`. Otherwise the terminals of close permutation traces satisfying their LogUp constraints telescope to the messages' nonzero fraction sum (`CompStark/Sound/Telescope.lean`), so the verifier rejects, or some instance has no close trace and permutation trace satisfying all its constraints, and the proof runs the single STARK's argument on it among the instances of its domain's size: the DEEP quotients of that size near on a common set make that instance's near too. So the knowledge error (`CompStark/Iop/BatchError.lean`) is `lookupError` plus the worst instance's `foldingError + outOfDomainError`, with its own list sizes and `identityDegree`, and batching of its size's opened words (`batchingErrorOf`, decoding losing two points of its domain), plus FRI's error with the shorter words injected (`friErrorWith`). FRI with inputs injected mid-folding is ArkLib's interaction-layer FRI certificate: a fold that injects a word is bad when the powers of its challenge over its `2 ^ k` coefficient words and the injected word make them agree with the code although they do not on a common set (the lossy correlated-agreement event of `2 ^ k + 1` words), and FRI rejects when any input is far; its error is `friErrorWith` (`friErrorWith_eq`). This covers every schedule Plonky3's verifier accepts: an instance at FRI's final length is injected by the last fold, at the final height, and with every instance there FRI does not fold and the queries check the batched words against the final polynomial (`Fri.Interaction.Schedule.Fits`, `Fri.Interaction.Schedule.plonky3`). The STARK's FRI is this FRI without injected inputs. A lookup's tuples are queries, of positive weight, or provided table entries, of weight `0` and any multiplicity (Plonky3's `Count::bounded` and `Count::provided`). Balance in the base field `ℤ/p` makes the queries included in what is provided: with the verifier's multiplicity bound `∑ᵢ wᵢ 2 ^ logNᵢ < p` (`Setup.HeightBounded`), one of its checks on every length vector it admits (`Statement.Admits`), and AIRs keeping every row's query multiplicities within their weights (`Air.CountsBounded`, Plonky3's `count_weight`, an obligation of the AIR the verifier does not check), for every bus and tuple of values some row of some instance provides it with a nonzero multiplicity (`Setup.Provides`, exposing the instance and row so the provider's AIR applies there), or its queries' multiplicities, read as the integers of magnitude below `p / 2` they stand for (`intValue`), sum to zero over `ℤ` (`Setup.Included`, `Sound.Batch.included_of_balanced`, `CompStark/Sound/Inclusion.lean`): without a provider, balance makes the queries' sum vanish modulo `p`, and the height bound keeps it below `p` in magnitude. So the knowledge error also bounds acceptance while no close tuple satisfies every AIR with lookup inclusion (`Setup.SatisfiedWithInclusion`):

  ```lean
  theorem batchKnowledgeSoundness_inclusion (C : Batch.Statement F)
      (publicValues : Fin C.k → List F) {OutP : (C.protocol EF).tree.ExecutionPath → Type}
      (prover : Interaction.Oracle.Prover.Strategy unifSpec (C.protocol EF).tree
        (C.protocol EF).roles OutP)
      (δ δ' : (Fin C.k → ℕ) → NNReal) (hδ' : ∀ n, C.Admits n → (δ' n : ℝ) < 1 - C.rate)
      (hcounts : ∀ n, C.Admits n → ∀ i, (C.air i).CountsBounded (n i)) :
      C.farAcceptance EF publicValues δ'
        (fun n traces ↦ (C.setup EF n).SatisfiedWithInclusion traces publicValues) prover ≤
        C.knowledgeError EF δ δ'
  ```

The `wideBus 64` batches pass it by `decide` (`wideBus_countsChecked`, `CompStark/Iop/LookupInclusion.lean`). - Security calculator (`CompStark/Calculator/Bounds.lean`): every error term above depends on the AIR only through a few numbers, its `Shape` (its constraint count and `identityDegree`, its opened words, whether it has lookups, its lookups' interactions `T` and widest tuple `W`), and on the heights and FRI's parameters. The calculator computes, for a setup, a base field of `p` elements, an MCA bound `B` and an `Analysis` (FRI's radius `δ`, the extractor's `δ' ≥ δ` below the Johnson radius `1 - √rate`, and a proximity loss, all rationals), a rational bound on each term of the knowledge error and of the round error, at any FRI arity: the bounds of `CompStark/Calculator/Numbers.lean`, with the list sizes at `δ'` at most `1` below the unique-decoding radius and the Johnson bound `⌊(1 - δ' - rate) / ((1 - δ')² - rate)⌋` above it (`listSize_le_listBound`), batching at the better of `B`'s count and the curve bound, and FRI's folds read off Plonky3's schedule as a plan of folds (ArkLib's `Fri.Interaction.Schedule.folds`: each fold's arity, the domain it folds onto, and whether it injects a word), each at the better of `B`'s count and the lossy bound. The MCA bound (`McaBound`, `CompStark/Calculator/McaBound.lean`) is computable data: where it admits a Reed–Solomon code of degree below `k` on `N` points at radius `δ`, a count of the exceptional seeds for power curves of degree `M`; `B.Holds` says that the counts bound the mutual correlated-agreement error of the powers over every finite field and domain. `McaBound.uniqueDecoding` counts `M N` within the unique-decoding radius, `2 ⌊δ N⌋ ≤ N - k` (`uniqueDecoding_holds`), `McaBound.johnson` counts `M` times the Dao–Kominers–Thaler exception count for lines ([DKT26] Theorem 5.12) up to the Johnson radius `1 - √ρ₋`, `ρ₋ = (k - 1) / N`, exactly as ArkLib's `johnsonExceptionCount` (`johnsonLineCount`: `(2μ - 1) h + θ (h + μ + 4 D μ h) + (n - D - 1) μ` with `μ = ⌈t / √ρ₋⌉ - 1`, `h = ⌈t² / (3 ρ₋)⌉ - 1`, `θ = (n - D) / (A - D)` at the agreement threshold `A = ⌈(1 - δ) N⌉` and the multiplicity `m = t - 1/2 = max(⌈r / (2 (1 - δ - r))⌉, 3)` for a dyadic `r ≥ √ρ₋`), in `Nat` and `ℚ` arithmetic with `⌈√·⌉` through `Nat.sqrt`, and `McaBound.uniqueOrJohnson` combines the two. `S.Secure p B A bits` (and `S.SecureRo p B A bits` per oracle answer, and the batch's) says that the analysis fits the setup, `B` admitting its batching and its folds, and the bound is at most `2 ^ (-bits)`; it is decidable in `Nat` and `ℚ` arithmetic, so `decide +kernel` checks it with the kernel evaluating the AIR's constraints, widths and lookups (a `wide 4096` table, 4096 constraints, takes about seven seconds, with `maxRecDepth` raised for its list of constraints). The analysis need not be picked by hand: `S.bestAnalysis p B` (and `bestAnalysisRo`, and the batch's) searches FRI's radius, the extractor's and the loss by coordinate descent over a grid for the smallest bound; it is computation only, so soundness rests on the check of what it finds, and every analysis of `CompStark/Calculator/Security.lean` is the search's. For a large AIR, `secure_of_shape` (and `secureRo_of_shape`) takes the shape as a fact proved once, and `shape_eq_of_profileFits` proves it from a one-pass kernel check of the constraints' degree profiles (`Air.ProfileFits`), so that each certificate evaluates only rationals. The round error's bound is the largest term, so its largest over the vectors is each term's: LogUp at every instance's longest admitted length, the instance terms at every instance's shape at every admitted length, batching on each domain size with every instance admitting it there, FRI's folds from every top length and at every input, and the queries at the most folds. The knowledge error sums its terms: at each top length every instance takes the length of the largest share, its LogUp interactions and, below the top, what its input adds to FRI's folds and queries, with the instance terms at their worst; that bounds every vector of that top length and is the per-vector bound of the vector it picks when the instance terms are worst there and its inputs sit at distinct heights, as for the `wideBus` batches, whose worst vector has every instance at the longest length. Every concrete bound above is such a check with the unique-decoding bound (`fibonacci_secure`, `wide_secure`, …, `wideBatchGrindStatement_secureRo`).

  What is proven and what is not: the list sizes up to the Johnson radius are proven (`CompStark/Sound/Johnson.lean`), so the extractor's `δ'` may go beyond the unique-decoding radius, with the folding and out-of-domain terms multiplied by the list sizes. FRI's soundness holds at every radius `δ` (ArkLib's `Fri.Interaction.acceptance_le`, Garreta–Mohnblatt–Wagner's round-by-round argument: each fold's MCA error, a curve of degree `2^k - 1`, or `2^k` when it injects an input, and `(1 - δ)^t` for the queries), and so does batching; the radius is limited by the MCA bound the calculator uses. Within the unique-decoding radius it is proven (`uniqueDecoding_holds`, from ArkLib's unique-decoding curve bound, with ArkLib's curve and lossy bounds in the unique-decoding range, `CodingTheory.UniqueDecoding`), so each query is worth at most $\log_2 (4/3) \approx 0.415$ bits at blowup 2 and $\log_2 (8/5) \approx 0.678$ at blowup 4. Up to the Johnson radius it is proven too (`johnson_holds`, `uniqueOrJohnson_holds`): ArkLib's Johnson-regime mutual correlated agreement for power curves (`ReedSolomon.exists_johnson_curve_exactPowerAgreement_line_mul_of_fintype`, Dao–Kominers–Thaler's line count with the curve degree as a factor, in any characteristic and on any domain, branch `fri-interaction` of the ArkLib fork, on ArkLib main), which brings a query up to $\log_2 (1/(1 - \delta))$ bits for `δ` up to `1 - √rate`, about 0.5 bits at blowup 2 and 1 at blowup 4.

  Plonky3's Keccak example (`tests/CompStarkTests/KeccakSecurity.lean`): `KeccakAir`, 3182 constraints of degree 3 over 2633 columns, every column opened at the next row, as a statement whose proofs may claim any trace length the verifier admits, $2^0$ to $2^{23}$ rows at blowup 2 and to $2^{22}$ at blowup 4, every certificate covering all of them at one analysis (`secureRo_of_lengths`, `secure_of_lengths`). ## Possible improvements

- Custom externs for the Poseidon2 kernels. Every `ByteArray` store in Lean carries an exclusivity check, so a loop over the eight lanes stays scalar, and clang's SLP vectorizer only handles one round at a time. Written as one loop over the lanes, the whole width-24 permutation keeps its state in registers and vectorizes cleanly: 211 ns in a C model of the Lean kernels, against 289 ns for the split Lean kernels and 179 ns for Plonky3. A check-free store primitive would unlock that shape, at the price of trusting an extern.
- Extending wide matrices across columns. The extension transforms one column at a time, vectorized along it, so its last layers shuffle within vectors, its inverse transform reverses bits, and the trace is transposed into columns first; Plonky3 transforms rows of the row-major matrix, eight columns per vector, with neither. On one thread the transforms are as fast as Plonky3's for a few dozen columns, but at Keccak-f's 2633 columns they take about 1.7 times its instructions (24.8 against 14.3 billion for $2^{16}$ rows, `CompStarkBench lde 16 2633 REPS`, `compstark-bench lde 16 2633 REPS`). Transforming strips of eight columns interleaved word by word, the trace's own layout, would remove the transposes, the shuffles and the reversal. Strips of eight interleaved columns, every butterfly between rows one vector with a broadcast twiddle, took that extension from 24.8 to 21.3 billion instructions, but its time on one thread from 2.15 to 2.35 s and on 16 threads from 0.30 to 0.75 s ($2^{18}$ rows of 64 columns: 0.042 to 0.15 s on 16 threads): a strip of $2^{16}$ rows is 2 MB, so its passes stream from L3 instead of L2, and sixteen at once exceed it. Transforming eight columns at once pays only with the passes blocked to the caches across columns and rows, as Plonky3 blocks its row-major matrix.
- Faster Poseidon2 kernels. Merkle hashing and grinding are most of a proof on one thread: on `wide 64` at $2^{16}$ rows the width-24 and width-16 permutations take about half the instructions of FRI's commit phase and of the commitments, at about 1.3 times Plonky3's time per permutation. Per group of eight width-16 states the kernels run about 13,300 instructions against 11,500 for Plonky3 (21,400 against 18,500 at width 24): a partial round about 276 against about 250, most of the difference the loads and stores of the state and the constants each round reloads, and a full round 880 against about 760, mostly in the S-box, whose Montgomery products the compiler widens to 64-bit lanes and narrows back where Plonky3 multiplies even and odd lanes in place, and whose constant additions are reduced where Plonky3 adds constants in `[-p, 0]` to signed products. Fusing round parts so that the state stays in locals does not pay with the compiler's SLP vectorizer: a full round in one kernel takes 2,150 instructions against 880 split, `mat4` with the block-sum mixing 823 against 385, two and four partial rounds per call 357 and 413 per round against 292, even with its recursion depth raised (`-mllvm -slp-recursion-max-depth`), because values shared between rows of one kernel are vectorized tree by tree and spilled; the mixing fused with the next S-boxes saves 31 of 635 instructions and no time. Interleaving two groups per call leaves the time per permutation unchanged, so the partial rounds are not latency bound. Carrying the state from round to round in the arguments of the partial-round loop instead of the buffer takes 357 instructions per round against 309 for the loop over the buffer: the state's 128 words are loop-carried scalars, which the vectorizer gathers and scatters every round. BabyBear's S-box `x ^ 7` as five products left in 64-bit lanes (`x ^ 2`, `x ^ 4`, `x ^ 5`, `x ^ 6`, `x ^ 7`, every lazy product below `2 ^ 32` for BabyBear), without the two reductions and narrowings between them, takes 1.2% fewer instructions per permutation and no less time. A full round's three kernels inlined into one function, each still a separate pass over the state, take 900 instructions against 938 for the three calls, in the same time.
- Hashing FRI's folded layers from their coordinate planes. Every folded layer is gathered into columns (`leaves`, every `k`-th word of a plane sixteen words at a time, `FoldWords.strided`) before it is hashed. Hashing the planes in place takes a leaf loader that reads a plane's pairs with a stride, beside `Merkle.Packed.hashColumns`'s loader of contiguous column lines, and a matrix type for strided columns. The arity-2 fold reads its pairs in place (`CompStark/Fri/FoldWords.lean`: `halves` and `diffs`, sixteen pairs per vectorized kernel, then `halves + β · diffs` on planes), which took the commit phase on `wide 64` from 1.02 to 0.89 billion instructions.

## Keccak hash kernels

`CompStark.Keccak` provides pure-Lean Keccak-f[1600] and original Keccak-256, with no new externs or inline C. `scripts/gen_keccak.py` generates the straight-line round in `Scalar.lean` and the sponge in `Sponge.lean`. Both retain 25 unboxed `UInt64` lanes across rounds; the sponge retains them across absorption too, with no boxed lane arrays or padded message allocation. Complete lanes load directly from the input through two approved `ByteArray.ugetUInt32LE` accesses. The final partial lane supplies the `0x01` delimiter, lane 16 supplies the final `0x80` bit, and eight approved `usetUInt32LE` stores write the 32-byte digest. Each 136-byte block, including the final padding block, executes one absorption transition and 24 rounds in a structurally recursive scalar loop. Both kernels use the packed-native library's `-march=native -mstackrealign` compilation flags.

`Refines.lean` proves each scalar round agrees with VCVio (`roundWith_eq`), then any number of rounds (`permute_eq`, `permute_succ_eq`) and the complete permutation (`f1600_eq`). The named sponge lemmas `loadLane_eq`, `partialLane_eq`, `lastLane_eq`, `digest_eq`, `absorbWith_eq`, `run_rounds_eq` and `run_blocks_eq` establish the packed accesses, padding and scalar transitions; `keccak256_eq` proves exact equality with `VCVio.Algorithms.Keccak.keccak256`. The public hash requires `input.size < USize.size`: the approved word accessor uses machine-sized offsets, while Lean's abstract `ByteArray` admits larger sizes. `keccak256Batch` takes the corresponding membership proof for every input and preserves input order, with its `_eq` refinement. These proofs are erased by compilation. The independent permutation API `f1600Batch` also preserves input order and uses the scalar kernel serially. `hashNode left right` hashes two 32-byte child digests in left/right order; `hashLeaf payload` hashes a 512-byte payload. Their existing length proofs discharge machine addressability, and `hashNode_eq`/`hashLeaf_eq` establish the reference digests. The complete hash and batch refinements depend only on Lean's standard logical axioms (`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`).

`tests/CompStarkTests/KeccakHash.lean` checks the empty and `abc` known answers, 26 lengths around byte/lane/rate boundaries and the 512-byte leaf size with four byte patterns, nonzero independent permutation states, both empty batches, and node/leaf framing. These checks run in the compiled test driver because the approved word accessors execute through their native implementations. The performance gate includes `keccak-scalar` (10,000 dependent permutations and 10,000 hashes of each message size) and `keccak-batch` (ten batches of 1,024 independent states/messages), with a phase for each operation and reference-checked sinks outside the measurements.

Build the Lean benchmark with `systemd-run --user --scope -p MemoryMax=12G -p MemorySwapMax=0 -- lake build CompStarkBench` and the pinned Plonky3 comparison with `cd bench/rust && cargo build --release --bin keccak-kernels`. The scalar initial state, patterned 64/512-byte messages, batch inputs and consumed 64-bit sinks match on both sides. Rust uses `black_box`, and its batch hashes use Plonky3's runtime-selected vector backend. The compiled Lean hash is called from a separate module, and every output contributes to the sink. Wall-clock runs hold the shared lock; the instruction counts from `scripts/perf-gate` decide regressions under host load.

### Batch vectorization and interleaving

Two- and four-state packed prototypes use structure-of-arrays storage at byte offset `(lane * states + state) * 8`, paired approved word accesses, and straight-line operations grouped across independent states. Separate scalar prototypes carry 50 or 100 unboxed lane parameters across all rounds. Lean's emitted C represents the lanes as individual `uint64_t` locals. With the bundled clang at `-O3 -march=native -mstackrealign`, all four hot bodies (`round2`, `round4`, `scalar2`, `scalar4`) contain zero packed arithmetic instructions and zero YMM register references. YMM copies appear only in interface wrappers. The four-state packed round uses scalar instructions such as:

```asm
69e: rorx $0x3f,%rbx,%rdx
6bc: rorx $0x3f,%rcx,%rcx
6e5: rorx $0x15,%rdx,%r8
724: rorx $0x3f,%r11,%rdx
76a: rorx $0x14,%r15,%rdi
780: andn %r8,%rdi,%rcx
```

In particular, its round arithmetic has no `vpxor`, `vpand`, `vpandn`, `vpsllq`, `vpsrlq` or `vpor`. ## Streaming Keccak Merkle commitments and overwrite duplex

`CompStark/Keccak/Merkle.lean` implements a binary root-only commitment to packed 64-bit rows. `Row` carries a complete, addressable leaf image in little-endian bytes, and `Row.ofWords` packs arbitrary lane values. The caller supplies lane permutations, zero prefixes and field-limb order. `Builder.absorb` consumes encoder tiles in row order, hashes each arriving row and merges completed subtrees by binary carry. `finish` rejects empty and incomplete trees. The returned `Subtree` owns both the complete leaf payloads and every cached digest; `Tree.root` is the public commitment. There is no cap, implicit leaf padding or protocol tag.

`Tree.nodes_eq` and `Tree.root_eq` identify the cache and commitment with VCVio's `buildMerkleTreeWithHash`, using VCVio's Keccak-256 for leaves and the concatenation of left and right digests for nodes. The cache certificate is erased at runtime. `carry_rows`, `Builder.absorb_rows`, `Builder.finish_rows` and `commit_rows` preserve the complete input payload sequence, including across tile boundaries. `Tree.open` sorts and deduplicates queries, returns their rows in ascending order, and uses VCVio's `MixedMerkleTree.prune` on cached authentication paths for the canonical bottom-up sibling stream. `Tree.open_eq` identifies every sibling with the reference tree's pruned paths. Empty or out-of-domain query sets are rejected. `verify` checks the row count, reconstructs the root with VCVio's walk and rejects leftover siblings. `Tree.batchProof` exposes the same retained cache through VCVio's intrinsic selector API; `Tree.batch_complete` proves honest multi-opening completeness.

`CompStark/Keccak/Duplex.lean` supplies an overwrite-duplex engine with a positive rate below 25 lanes, a bounded pending cursor, and a generic observer accumulator. Absorption overwrites rate lanes; a full rate flushes when another lane arrives. Every sample performs a fresh permutation, returns the requested output prefix and zeroes the rate while retaining capacity. The observer receives every exact permutation input and output in execution order. `Writer.write` transmits and binds one caller-framed message together; `Writer.sample` leaves the transmitted stream unchanged. `absorb_eq`, `sample_eq` and `Writer.write_eq` prove complete state, observer and output equality with the same engine over VCVio's Keccak-f. Framing and tags are supplied by the caller. `grind` probes a bounded interval in increasing order, observes only the accepted transition and returns exhaustion explicitly. `findNonce_min` proves minimality, and `grind_eq` proves equality with VCVio. Grinding is serial; the generic engine imposes no field or nonce encoding.

The tree cases cover one, two, eight and 32 leaves; widths 1, 8, 16, 17, 34 and 64 words; duplicate and unsorted queries, all leaves and single leaves. Native negative checks cover missing rows, surplus siblings, empty and out-of-range queries, empty commitments and non-power-of-two row counts. The duplex fixtures cover empty samples, exact rate fills, automatic flushes, repeated samples and full capacity carry. Axiom reports for the refinement and completeness theorems permit only Lean's standard logical axioms.

## Shared-challenge preparation
