# Binary-field multilinear stack

The components of a multilinear proof system over the binary field `BF64` and its cubic extension, as leanMultisig's leanVM uses them: the Keccak overwrite-duplex transcript and Keccak-256 Merkle commitment, a polynomial commitment from stacked additive Reed–Solomon codewords, ring switching and a WHIR opening, and the Keccak-f GKR argument. Each has a specification, a prover, a verifier and a soundness analysis; each fast implementation is proved equal to its specification. The tests compare them with leanMultisig `9d8bce818aa7c7e18e67026e522a9b1539d0a57d`.

None of these components is at parity with the Rust reference. The table gives the measured gap where the Lean code and the workload match a Rust measurement, and says where a component is unmeasured.

| Component | Workload | Lean / Rust, single-thread instructions |

| Merkle commitment (`Keccak.Merkle`) | 4,096 rows of 64 words; 100 queries ([components](components.md#streaming-keccak-merkle-commitments-and-overwrite-duplex)) | 4.03× commit, 0.72× open |
| Duplex writer (`Keccak.Duplex`) | 10,000 messages written and sampled; an eight-bit grind ([components](components.md#streaming-keccak-merkle-commitments-and-overwrite-duplex)) | 2.88× write and sample, 2.99× grind |
| PCS prover kernels (`Pcs.RingSwitch`, `Pcs.Tensor`) | Six-map coordinates, 64 packed bit folds, 256 mapped weights ([binary PCS](binary-pcs.md#kernel-measurements)) | 8.58×, 3.84×, 7.27× |

The Keccak-f GKR count is the `keccak-gkr` performance-gate workload, 2.31 billion instructions. The Rust counts are single-thread user-space instruction counts of leanMultisig `9d8bce818aa7c7e18e67026e522a9b1539d0a57d` on an AMD Ryzen 7 3700X, not re-measured here: 291 million for its `Keccak reduction` span on the same 512 permutations, run on its own after the output claims are bound, and 218 million for the same span inside the prover of a complete Fibonacci proof.

leanVM's complete proof system, measured the same way on the same host and revision, opens its PCS at 22× the Rust instructions and verifies at 4.6–4.9× (72–77 ms against 13 ms on the SET and Fibonacci programs); that opening driver and verifier are leanVM's composition and not part of this library.

## Reproducing the Rust fixtures

`scripts/check-binary-stack-reference /path/to/leanMultisig` builds the harnesses `tests/rust/keccak-gkr.rs`, `binary-pcs.rs`, `keccak-transport.rs` and `keccak-transcript.rs` against an unchanged checkout of leanMultisig `9d8bce818aa7c7e18e67026e522a9b1539d0a57d`, regenerates every fixture of `tests/data/keccak-gkr`, `binary-pcs`, `keccak-transport` and `keccak-transcript` with one and with sixteen Rust workers, and compares them byte for byte with the committed files; `--write` replaces them. The Keccak-f GKR fixture and the ring-switching slices are computed from `tests/data/keccak-gkr/active/committed.bin`, the 64-lane Keccak region of a leanVM execution with 512 padded permutations (input and middle lanes and nonzero metadata in the spare lanes); that input is data, not regenerated.

## Fields

`CompStark/BinaryFields` fixes `K = BF64` and `E = BF64.Ext3` from CompPoly, the extension's limbs, and a kernel-checked certificate that `g` generates `K`'s multiplicative group (`Generator`). `Native` and `WordProduct` replace the field operations in compiled code with CompPoly's word arithmetic and the five-product cubic multiplication, each by a `csimp` theorem equating them with the specification instances.

## Keccak transcript and Merkle commitment

The specifications are `Keccak.Transcript` (`Keccak/Transcript/Duplex.lean`): a sixteen-lane overwrite duplex absorbing tagged extension scalars and nonces, squeezing one extension element per permutation, seeding from two digests and grinding by a discarded squeeze; and `Keccak.Commit` (`Keccak/Commit`): binary Keccak-256 trees over leaf images of field words, pruned multi-openings, their masked extraction and node-level binding (`disagreement_bound`), and the random-oracle trace of commitment and challenge queries with its exception bound (`RandomOracleTrace.bad_probability_le`). `Correspondence` proves the transcript's framing and replay, and `Lanes` maps lanes to field words. `Keccak.Commit.Codec` and `MerkleCodec` give the exact little-endian encodings of words, scalars, leaves and child pairs; `Compiled.MerkleTrace` recovers every checked path from a lazily sampled Keccak oracle's log and bounds the trace's exceptions by `(max 2 roots + 1) Q² / 2^256` (`exception_probability_le`), and `ChallengeSnapshot` with `FreshSnapshot` and `FreshOracleLog` proves the recovery independent of each later challenge answer.

The fast implementations are CompStark's lane-level `Keccak.Duplex` and streaming `Keccak.Merkle`. `Keccak.TranscriptDuplex` (`Keccak/Transcript/Native.lean`) proves the writer equal to the specification: `absorb_spec`, `absorbAll_spec` and `sample_spec` at every item, including full-rate flushes, and `grind_refines` and `grind_min` for smallest-nonce grinding. `Keccak.MerkleLeaves.merkleRow_hash` identifies the streaming tree's leaf hash with the commitment's leaf digest.

`tests/CompStarkTests/KeccakTranscript.lean` checks 48 Rust vectors: seeds, squeezes across rate boundaries, repeated empty squeezes, digest absorption, grinding checks and pruned openings. `tests/CompStarkTests/KeccakTransport.lean` checks smallest-nonce grinding at 0, 5 and 9 bits and Merkle roots, rows and hints on field-word leaves.

## Polynomial commitment

`Pcs.BinaryDomain` fixes the basis `1, g, …, g^63` and the binary additive domain; `Pcs.AdditiveEncoding` proves ArkLib's `Whir.EncodingContract` for the novel-basis additive encoding over `K` and `E` for `vars + rate ≤ 64` (`encodingContract`, `extensionEncodingContract`), and `computableAdditiveNTT_eq_encode` its agreement with CompPoly's executable additive NTT below 64. `Pcs.Prover.Encoding.encode` runs the native bit-plane encoder on extension rows.

Ring switching (`Pcs.RingSwitch.Protocol`) reduces 64 bit-slice claims at an extension point to one weighted claim on the base-field stack through six Frobenius maps, with its knowledge certificate, local bounds and terminal identity (`terminal_iff`); `Pcs.RingSwitch.Prover` computes the honest slices and target. `Pcs.Honest.prove` commits a base-field table and opens it with ArkLib's WHIR against explicit verifier coins, and `proveRing` batches the ring-switched claim into the same opening; `prove_accepts` and `proveRing_accepts` prove completeness for every schedule and every true claim. `Pcs.Verifier` gives cached refinements of WHIR's inner products, evaluations and acceptance.

The extractor decodes with CompPoly's Guruswami–Sudan decoder at exact integer Johnson cutoffs: `Pcs.Radius` computes the cutoff (`agreementCutoff_le_iff`), `Pcs.Parameters` selects multiplicities, and `Pcs.Decoder.gsListDecoder_correct` proves the interleaved list decoder correct with the real Johnson list bound. `Pcs.Extraction` and `Pcs.ExtractorCache` prove the cached extractor equal to ArkLib's WHIR extractor at every level (`openingExtractorCached_eq`, `levelExtractorCached_eq`).

`Pcs.Errors` lists WHIR's error per verifier message. `Pcs.Levels` reads a schedule's levels as natural numbers and decides rational bounds on every row in the kernel: `whirErrorRows_le` turns `levelsCheck κ κq` into `2^-κ` on every algebraic row and `2^-κq` on every column-query row, for any schedule, rate and dimension, the Johnson radius and MCA slack measured at the reduced rate. `Pcs.Schedule` is leanMultisig's ladder (initial fold 6, then 4, rate exponents `r, r + 3, …`, its query table and grinding), and `Pcs.ProductionLevels.productionLevels_check` certifies all 56 of its configurations at `2^-128` algebraic and `2^-111` per column query, `2^-128` after 17 bits of query grinding.

`tests/CompStarkTests/BinaryPcs.lean` checks cube indexing, the Johnson cutoffs and basis conversion. `tests/CompStarkTests/BinaryPcsNative.lean` checks that the honest opening is accepted and an inconsistent claim rejected, and compares the native extension encoding (98,304 bytes) and the six-map ring-switching target with Rust.

## Keccak-f GKR

`Keccak.Gkr` is a public-coin reduction from claims on the outputs of `2^(p+4)` Keccak-f permutations to claims on their inputs and committed middle states: twelve layers per segment of two twelve-round segments, the nonlinear layer through the sixteen-coordinate reverse multiplication-friendly embedding of `Keccak.Embedding` (injectivity and the CRT correspondence kernel-checked), pack and position sumchecks and the linear layer by its transpose. `Gkr.soundness` bounds acceptance for a wrong input table by `(37 (p + 4) + 902) / |E|` over any characteristic-two field `E`; `Gkr.certificate` is its knowledge certificate on ArkLib's interaction layer.

`Keccak.GkrProver.prove` is the word-level prover. `specificationTranscript_complete` proves the specification prover accepted for every challenge sequence; `prove_eq_specificationTranscript` and `prove_buildWitness_eq_specificationTranscript` prove the fast prover equal to it, and `prove_complete` and `prove_buildWitness_complete` transfer completeness. `tests/CompStarkTests/KeccakGkr.lean` compares every witness layer, transmitted scalar, challenge, batch claim and opening with Rust on 512 permutations, and the `keccak-gkr` performance-gate workload counts the reduction.

## Grand-product GKR

`Multilinear.GrandProduct` proves three batched product trees over any leaves: an odd root-most binary layer followed by radix-four layers, each a sumcheck of normalized messages carrying the cofactor of the known equality factor, then child interpolation. Its knowledge certificate carries riders, constraint-residual tables over an arbitrary witness (`Riders W`: a fit predicate, tables of fixed heights below a common bound, and their values on the Boolean rows); intermediate layers keep every rider zero on its Boolean rows, and the final layer restricts each rider at its own challenges. `GrandProductDecision` decides the riders by their finite mixed Boolean grid (`ridersRestricted_iff_grid`) and every layer relation, and `GrandProductBadEvents` bounds each normalized round and child interpolation of a fixed candidate list by its local error. `WordGrandProduct` evaluates the layers on machine words. `tests/CompStarkTests/GrandProduct.lean` checks layer schedules, layer claims and rider decisions. The prover is not part of the library and the component is unmeasured.

## Multilinear algebra and composition

`Multilinear.Basic` defines low-bit-first multilinear extensions over `E` with their word-level evaluators; `Multilinear.Algebra` holds their linearity and interpolation, stacking, fingerprints, product trees and fixed-polynomial sumcheck bounds. `Multilinear.Retained` gives identity backward maps on concrete transcript trees. `Compose.ListLift` lifts a family of knowledge certificates over a candidate list fixed before any challenge, multiplying each error by the real list bound, and `Compose.Reduction` composes such reductions sequentially and appends an opening.

## Tables and buses

`CompStark/Tables` describes a statement for this stack: a `System` is a list of tables connected by named buses. A table is a `Stark.Air` whose constraints read only the current row of its trace and of its preprocessed trace (`Expr.currentRow`, unlike `Expr.rowLocal` of lookups, which also admits the next row and public values), with its bus interactions (on every executed row, push or pull the tuple of its expressions on a bus), the columns that must not vanish on executed rows, its virtual columns, bound by the system's `external` relation that other arguments establish, and its public padding row. The system lists its buses; the `i`-th is separated by `g ^ i`. A `Layout` gives each table its executed rows and its power-of-two height; rows past the executed count are padding: they hold arbitrary data that satisfies the constraints and take no part in buses or nonzero columns, and the bus argument corrects each by the public padding row, so a table with padding rows reads no preprocessed column in its tuples. Public data is preprocessed, so a data source that is not a table of the computation is a table whose tuple coordinates are preprocessed or committed columns. `System.Satisfied` is the relation; `System.WellFormed` the decidable side conditions. The description reuses `Stark.Expr` and `Stark.Air` (width, constraints, preprocessed columns) so that one AIR serves the FRI backend and this one, but not `Air.lookups`: their signed field-valued multiplicities are Plonky3's LogUp, which has no direction or natural multiplicity in characteristic two, so interactions carry a direction and multiplicities are natural through repeated tuples.

The bus argument starts from one multiset equality: every tuple is encoded with its bus's separator first, padded to `2 ^ k` entries, and under `BusShape` (one width per bus) every bus balances exactly when the encoded pushes and pulls are equal (`System.balanced_iff`); an unbalanced system passes the grand-product test at a uniform `(α, β)` with probability at most `max 1 k · cap / |E|` (`System.unbalanced_collision_le`). The three trees of the grand-product GKR read `System.leaves`: one block per interaction or nonzero column, executed rows giving `β` minus a fingerprint or the column's value and padding rows that form corrected by the padding row's, stacked in decreasing height; with every padding row the padding row, `root_eq_sideProduct` identifies the pushed and pulled roots with the grand products of the encoded multisets, and `nonzeroRoot_ne_zero_iff` the nonzero root with the nonzero columns. `System.riders` gives the constraint residuals as the GKR's riders, which vanish exactly when every constraint holds on every row (`ridersZero_iff`).

The bus protocol (`Tables/BusProtocol.lean`) draws `(α, β)`, receives the pushed and nonzero roots, runs the grand-product GKR over the three sides at the common depth `System.depth` (the largest side, the tallest table and at least one variable) with the constraint residuals as riders, and ends with the boundary values. A table is opened directly (`System.Direct`) when it has no constraints, no nonzero columns and no padding rows and its tuple entries are columns, preprocessed columns or constants; the committed columns its tuples read are the boundary columns, sent by the prover, and every other table's selector-weighted bus forms become one debt per side for the table sumcheck (`leaf_decomposition`: a side's leaf extension at the GKR point is the public part, computed from the boundary values, the preprocessed columns and the padding rows, plus the table debt). `Bus.certificate` is the knowledge certificate on `GrandProduct.certificate`, with the witness retained; `Bus.knowledgeSoundness` bounds extraction failure by `Bus.error = (max 1 k · 2 ^ depth + GKR error numerator) / |E|`, the first term the fingerprint collision of an unbalanced system (`unbalanced_root_collision_le`), and `Bus.unbalanced_acceptance_le` bounds a true endpoint for an unbalanced witness by the same error.

The table sumcheck (`Tables/TableSumcheck.lean`) covers the tables that are not opened directly. It draws `ξ` and batches, with consecutive powers, every constraint of every table in table order (directly opened tables have none) and the three bus debts, into one sumcheck over the tallest such table's variables, highest variable first; each table weighs its rows by the equality weight at the bus point on its own variables and by the product of the variables above its height. Rounds send compressed messages of degree `TableSumcheck.degree + 1`, where `degree` is the largest individual degree of a constraint or tuple entry, at least one; at the end the prover sends each table's transmitted columns, those its constraints, tuples or nonzero checks read, at the final point. `TableSumcheck.certificate` is the knowledge certificate from the bus endpoint, and `TableSumcheck.knowledgeSoundness` bounds failure by `TableSumcheck.error = (constraintCount + 2 + (degree + 1) · height) / |E|`: the `ξ` batch is a polynomial of degree `constraintCount + 2` in `ξ` (`debtPolynomial_eval`), and each round costs `degree + 1`. `Tables/Argument.lean` runs the two in sequence: a witness that satisfies the external relation but not the system reaches true final claims with probability at most `Bus.error + TableSumcheck.error` (`Argument.unsatisfied_acceptance_le`), and at accepted endpoints the committed witness satisfies the system unless one of the two extractors failed (`Argument.paths_satisfied_or_bad`). The final claims, the boundary values and the transmitted values at their points, are the openings the commitment owes.

leanVM's counted lookups are this pattern. A source, memory or bytecode, is a table opened directly: its address and public data are preprocessed columns, its committed data and its finalize count are committed columns, and it pushes `(address, 1, data)` and pulls `(address, final, data)` on one bus. Every reading row pulls `(address, c, data)` and pushes `(address, c · g, data)`, and declares `c` a nonzero column; the nonzero blocks are leanVM's count blocks and the nonzero tree its count tree, whose leaves are the count columns' values with the padding correction and whose nonzero root shows every executed count nonzero. With counts nonzero, fewer reads than the order of `g` and balance, each read returns its slot's data and `final = g ^ reads`; that cap is a condition on the layout and the conclusion a lemma of the instance, not part of the system. The state bus is a height-zero table opened directly whose tuples are the public initial and final states as constants. The `program` example is a bytecode source with its reading table, and its tests check that the program table is opened directly at its final counts.

The bus and table sumcheck are leanVM's choices stated for a general system: separators `g ^ i` in bus order, the public padding row, one GKR over three sides at a common depth, sorted blocks with selector weights, and one `ξ` batch and one sumcheck over the tallest table with back-loaded shorter tables. leanVM's protocol is an instance when its tables are declared in its table order, each with its columns in production order, the memory table before the bytecode table with its limbs before its finalize column (the boundary values are the directly opened tables' read committed columns, table by table, columns increasing), the tallest table has a block on some side and some side has at least two leaves (then `System.depth` is leanVM's `busDepth`), and every table column other than Keccak's committed middle-state lanes is read by a constraint, a tuple or a nonzero check (the table sumcheck transmits exactly the read columns); that these hold for leanVM's tables is not checked here. The Fiat–Shamir transcript, the commitment and the fast verifier are the composed system below.

`CompStark/Tables/Examples.lean` has two systems over `BF64`, proved well formed: `squares` (three tables of different heights, a tuple pulled twice, a virtual column) and `program` (a public program as a preprocessed source with padding, read by an execution table through counts that must be nonzero); `tests/CompStarkTests/Tables.lean` checks their witnesses, rejects tampered ones, and checks which tables the bus opens directly, the boundary columns, the transmitted columns and the sumcheck's degree and height.

## Composed proof system

`CompStark/Tables/Composed` composes the bus and the table sumcheck with the stacked commitment, ring switching and the WHIR opening, for any `System` and `Layout`. The committed object is the stack (`System.stack`): every committed column in decreasing height, each an aligned block of a table on `M` variables. The verifier's state is that stack; a `Setup` adds the side conditions (well formed, bus shape, the stack fits, the boundary columns are committed, the table sumcheck's degree fits the interpolation points) and an `External` argument for the virtual columns. An `External` says how a witness is read off a stack (`interpret`), carries a rider on the table sumcheck's draws (a relation the table phase restricts one coordinate at a time, so that the argument starts at the table's point instead of drawing its own), gives its start relation at the table sumcheck's output, its protocol, its parsed output and its weighted claims on the stack; `External.Reductions` gives its reductions and error and `External.Honest` its honest prover. `External.none` serves a system without virtual columns.

The outer reductions run the bus, the table sumcheck and the external argument on the stack; their outputs are pooled into weighted stack claims (`openingClaims`: the boundary values, the transmitted committed values, the argument's claims), which the WHIR opening checks. `Composed.knowledgeSoundness` bounds extraction failure of the composed certificate by `totalError = initialListBound · (Bus.error + TableSumcheck.error + external error) + whirBudget`, the WHIR errors from `Pcs`' certified levels, and `extracted_satisfied` turns extracted knowledge into a satisfying witness. `Composed.completeness` shows the honest prover's transcript accepted against every coin sequence for every satisfying witness whose padding rows are the padding row. `Composed.verifyWith` is the executable verifier, with certified evaluators for every claim weight, and `verify_iff` proves it equal to the specification's acceptance.

The Keccak table (`Composed/Keccak`) is the external argument for virtual output lanes: a table of `2 ^ (p + 4)` permutations whose 64 committed slots are stacked as one aligned region and whose 25 output lanes are virtual. Its rider is the Keccak-f GKR's initial discrepancy on the instance coordinates; after the table sumcheck it runs the GKR without its instance draws, at the table's point, then six ring-switching draws on the slot region, leaving a word claim and one ring-switched claim. Its error is `(36 (p + 4) + 902) / |E| + 6 / 2 ^ 192`. Its honest prover is the GKR's specification transcript; the compiled tests use the native GKR prover, whose transcript is the specification's at its own challenges.

`CompStark/Compiled` and `Tables/Compiled` compile the composed system: the stack's initial word and WHIR's encoded words as Keccak Merkle roots, every coefficient message revealed, draws from the Keccak duplex seeded with an initialization vector and a statement digest. `Tables.Compiled.knowledgeSoundness` is standard-model knowledge soundness: a proof verifies while the extracted stack fails the composed input relation with probability at most the Merkle extractor's node failures plus, at each of the `count` draws, the correlation-intractability failure of the challenge table. The random-oracle bounds on those games are not instantiated for tables. `Tables.Compiled.check` is the executable verifier of a compiled proof, one duplex traversal checking every challenge and grinding nonce (`WireObserver.check`, `Presentation.grindingCheck`) and then the Merkle openings and the fast composed verifier on the opened path; `check_iff` proves it decides `Tables.Compiled.verify`. It reads only the computable `Compiled.Data` of the parameters (`check_data`). `Data.prove` is the honest non-interactive prover: it drives a `Presentation.Prover` (`Compiled/Prover.lean`) through the presentation with the duplex (`Presentation.run`), the bus, table-sumcheck and WHIR provers (`Tables/Compiled/Prover.lean`, `Compiled/GrandProductProver.lean`, `Compiled/WhirProver.lean`) materializing every message as data, the Keccak table's prover running the native Keccak-f GKR prover with its writer seeded by the duplex state at the GKR's first move (`Tables/Compiled/KeccakProver.lean`), and attaches the honest openings at the WHIR queries. `Pcs.evaluatedLadder` certifies the evaluated schedules of the fixtures with the production Johnson choices. `Tables.Compiled.completeness` proves that every proof `Data.prove` produces for a satisfying, padded witness is accepted, for a schedule sampling a query per level and an external prover whose ideal runs follow an honest strategy (`ExternalFollows`): the compiled prover's ideal run (`Presentation.idealRun`) is the composed honest transcript at the drawn coins (`prover_idealRun`, from `bus_idealRun`, `table_idealRun` and `whir_idealRun`), its rows decode to its words and commit to its roots, and the honest batches agree with the complete words, so the composed completeness transfers to the opened path. `programCompiled_complete` is the `program` system's instance. The extractors are computable definitions separate from the certificates' probability-valued schedules and errors: `Bus.extractor`, `Composed.extractor` over the outer reductions' backward maps (`outerMaps`, `certificate_extractor`) and `Compiled.extractor`; a `Parameters` value holds them next to its reductions' schedules, so the extractor of a concrete instance runs only from data supplied without those fields. `Tables.Compiled.knowledgeSoundness_listed` is the list form: the adversary outputs, next to its proof, the hash inputs it computed (leaf payloads and child pairs, a `Listing`), the Merkle extractor is the dictionary of that list (`dictionary`: a digest's first listed input), and the Merkle failure term is the probability of `Unexplained hash params`, a revealed hash input that hashes to its node's target but is not the dictionary's input for that target, one definition generic in the hash.

`fixtures/CompStarkFixtures/Tables.lean` runs the `program` system and `fixtures/CompStarkFixtures/TablesKeccak.lean` the `hashes` system (a Keccak table of 256 permutations with virtual output lanes, read by a user table that pulls one state twice) through the honest prover and the fast verifier; `tests/CompStarkTests/TablesComposed.lean` and the compiled `tests/CompStarkTests/TablesKeccak.lean` check acceptance and rejection. `tests/CompStarkTests/TablesCompiled.lean` runs both systems compiled: the honest proofs are accepted, proofs with a tampered Merkle root, grinding nonce or opening row are rejected, and so is the proof of a prover that sends a wrong first table-sumcheck message and otherwise follows the duplex. The `tables` performance-gate workload proves and verifies the `program` system; `tables-compiled` proves and checks both systems compiled. Checking the compiled `hashes` proof (256 Keccak-f permutations) costs 490 million instructions, about 70 ms on one thread, and the `program` proof 10 million, 2 ms. Of the `hashes` check, the Keccak-f GKR's layer and opening checks take about 40%: they are decided on the three-word carrier `BF64.WordExt3`, the transcript mapped into it once (`KeccakTable.acceptsWord`, equal to the specification's decision by `Keccak.Gkr.map_accepts_iff`), with each claim's position weights and equality tables materialized. The opening claims' certified weight evaluators (the bus and table pool and ring switching) take about 25%, parsing the bus and table-sumcheck endpoints about 15%, the duplex replay and the grinding test about 13%, and WHIR's own checks and the Merkle batches the rest. The external argument's checks are decided apart from its claims (`External.accepts`), so the verifier never materializes the ring-switched weight over the stack; arithmetic on `E` itself, a vector of `Nat`-backed `BF64` limbs, allocates and reaches GMP on large limbs, which is why the hot checks run on the word carrier.
